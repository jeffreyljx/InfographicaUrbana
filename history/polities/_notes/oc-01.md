## Per state: what could not be settled

- kingdom-of-tahiti: start 1788 (Pōmare I page) with the land named without a date; the 1808-1815 loss and regain of Tahiti is dated by the Kingdom page only (22 December 1808; 12 November 1815) and names no rebel leader. The French protectorate (9 September 1842) and the November 1843 annexation are France's relation: the French dossiers (bourbon-kingdom-of-france to 1847, french-second-republic, second-french-empire, french-third-republic) should carry vassal_start 1842 / vassal_end 1880 with counterpart kingdom-of-tahiti. The Leeward kingdoms (Raiatea-Tahaa, Huahine, Bora Bora) were outside the kingdom (Leewards War page: annexed 16-19 March 1888, Huahine ceded 11 September 1895, Chamber ratified 19 November 1897) and have no dossier. Tuamotus, Mehetiʻa and Tetiʻaroa: no dated accession on any page. The Kingdom page dates the war 1843-1847, the war page 1844-1847.
- kingdom-of-bau / tovata-confederacy: the Wikipedia pages are stubs (no Bau-Rewa wars, no Kaba date, no territory). Bau starts 1852 (Cakobau Vunivalu, 8 December 1852), becomes Tui Bau 2 May 1867, merges into the Kingdom of Fiji in June 1871; Tovata is conceived 14 February 1867, recognised in Cakaudrove and Bua in May 1869; Macuata and the 1871 adhesion are bridged (the Kingdom of Fiji page is silent on Maʻafu's adhesion). The Confederacy of Independent Kingdoms of Viti (8 May 1865 - 2 May 1867) is an association (umbrella) and is not drawn. Burebasaga (Rewa, Nadroga, Serua, Kadavu) has no dossier: its page has no dated history before 2000.
- kingdom-of-samoa: 1873 to 16 February 1900 (monarchy abolished under the Tripartite Convention, signed 2 December 1899). Tutuila is in the shape at confidence 2 (the page lists American Samoa as successor); Manuʻa (separate chiefdom, cession deed 6 July 1904) is excluded and needs its own dossier but no page dates its beginning. The pre-1873 districts, the tafaʻifa and the Faitasiga confederacy are not on any page opened. Mataʻafa's 1899 rebel government (capital Aele) is not drawn.
- united-tribes-of-new-zealand: 28 October 1835 to the 21 May 1840 proclamation of British sovereignty; Northland is a stand-in for "the north of the North Island". The Treaty of Waitangi (February 1840) and the 2014 Waitangi Tribunal finding are noted, not resolved.
- maori-king-movement: 1858 (coronation June 1858) to 1883 ("by 1883 the King Country was made accessible"). The Waikato (Hamilton 50 km) is lost in April 1864 and the King Country (a 55 km circle at 175.1 E, 38.6 S minus the Hamilton circle) is gained from nobody in the same month. The Tāwhiao page was opened for the king's residence 1864-1883 and names none (itinerant for twenty years). The Kīngitanga continues as an institution without territory.
- rapa-nui: start 1200 is a bridging choice (pages give 300-400, 700-800, 800, 400-1300, 1200 CE); no ariki mau list, no capital. Ends 9 September 1888 (Agreement of Wills).
- pohnpei-chiefdoms: Saudeleur c. 1100 to Isokelekel c. 1628 (rename), Nahnmwarki era to the Spanish foundation of Santiago de la Ascensión "by 1886"; the five chiefdoms are not named or separated on any page opened.
- yapese-empire: c. 950 (seat of the Sawei empire) to 1899 (sale to Germany), the end at confidence 1; the outer-island tribute network has no land cells at the grid and is not drawn. The Yap page's "Spanish colony from the 17th century until 1899" is left unreconciled.
- kingdom-of-maui / kingdom-of-kauai: the pre-unification Hawaiian realms. "Kingdom of Maui" did not load; the dossier rests on the Kahekili II, Kalanikūpule, Kamakahelei, Kaumualiʻi and Unification of Hawaii pages. The 1794 split (Maui and Molokaʻi to Kāʻeokūlani, aliʻi of Kauaʻi, July; his death 12 December 1794) is a loss/gain pair between the two dossiers. Pages disagree on whether Kamehameha took Maui in 1794 or 1795, and on Kaumualiʻi's accession (1794 or 1795). Oʻahu before 1783 (Kahahana), Keōua's Kaʻū and Puna and Keawemaʻuhili's Hilo (all 1790-1791) have no dossier.
