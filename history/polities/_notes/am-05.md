## puebloans-chaco-regional-system: Chaco Canyon regional system (Ancestral Puebloans) (850..1151)

Type 'culture' (not 'state'): the pages call Chaco a 'major cultural center' and 'Chacoan culture' and never a state, polity or chiefdom; Cliopatria and Seshat (seed ids us_chaco_1, us_chaco_2) code it as a polity. A state of a kind is not shown by the pages opened, so the renderer should draw it muted. Page budget 1-2: two pages opened (Ancestral Puebloans, Chaco Culture National Historical Park). Wikidata Q478805 is shared with puebloans-aztec-regional-system; the two are kept as two dossiers because Cliopatria and Seshat carry them as separate systems (us_chaco_1/2 versus us_aztec_puebloan), the Chaco shape ends in 1151 with the canyon sealed, and the Aztec shape (8,739 km2) is a northern remnant starting 1152; the pages themselves do not say whether Aztec is a continuation or a successor, and treat both as Ancestral Puebloans, so a reviewer may merge them under this slug with both Names. The Chaco shape of 1040-1151 matches the San Juan Basin area (65,000 km2) given on the page. The Cliopatria 1040 start is not found on the page (it dates a boom 1020-1050 and the beams 1033-1092); the 1151 end is one year past the page's 1150. No ruler list, no wars, no names of counterparts: the pages name no neighbouring state. Regions: Chaco Canyon is searched as 'Pueblo Bonito' (a gazetteer place, -107.96 36.06).

## puebloans-aztec-regional-system: Aztec Ruins regional system (Ancestral Puebloans) (1152..1304)

Type 'culture' for the same reason as the Chaco dossier: the pages name no state. Wikidata Q478805 is shared with puebloans-chaco-regional-system; kept as a separate dossier (Cliopatria and Seshat code a separate Aztec system, ids us_aztec_puebloan versus us_chaco_1/2, with a different and much smaller shape starting the year after Chaco's), but the pages treat both as the Ancestral Puebloans and say the Aztec Ruins were built as a Chaco north outlier from 1100, so a reviewer may merge them under the Chaco slug with both Names. Page budget 1-2: only the Aztec Ruins National Monument page was opened for this state beyond the two shared pages, and it is thin (no abandonment, no extent, no mention of Chaco or Mesa Verde); both shapes are therefore unverified and both events are confidence 1 ('page silent'). A gap-filler should open the Mesa Verde National Park, Salmon Ruins and Seshat pages for the 1150-1300 dates (the gazetteer's places Mesa Verde and Farmington both fall inside the shape, which the Aztec Ruins page does not cover). The Aztec Ruins site is not in the gazetteer's places; the capital uses the page's coordinates. Gazetteer names needed: place 'Aztec Ruins' (36.836 N, 107.998 W).

## haudenosaunee: Haudenosaunee Confederacy (1450..1784)

Nine pages opened (budget up to 9 for 24 shapes). The Haudenosaunee Confederacy is alive today (45,000 enrolled in Canada and 81,000 in the United States in 2010, per the Iroquois page); end is set to the 1784 Treaty of Fort Stanwix because Cliopatria draws no shape after 1782 and the pages describe the homeland passing under the United States (Paris 1783, Fort Stanwix 1784); the Cliopatria 1781 shape is extended to 1784 for that reason. Honest limits: (1) the Ohio/Michigan/Illinois 'territory' of 1659-1701 (up to 570,382 km2) is a hunting-ground claim by conquest, left depopulated by the wars (Beaver Wars page); the renderer may want it lighter than the homeland, and the Nanfan deed of 1701 and the 1768 cession name the same land; no page calls any people a vassal of the Haudenosaunee, so no vassal events are recorded (rule 4); (2) from 1683 to 1687 the shapes' growth and from 1734 to 1769 the contraction of the rim are not explained by any page opened (events at confidence 1 with the counterpart read from which Cliopatria shape covers the lost cells); (3) the 1726 strip on Lakes Erie and Ontario and the 1768 Kentucky and West Virginia cession name land the Cliopatria shapes never draw since 1702: they are captions, not cell changes; (4) the French contest is not recorded because the pages show raids and expeditions, not ground held (Lachine, La Prairie, Trois-Rivieres, Denonville 1687, 1692); (5) cell changes below 1% of the area (1718, 1741, 1762, 1775, 1776) carry no event; (6) the Great Peace of Montreal of 4 August 1701 has no ledger event of its own (no type fits a treaty with no territorial term) and sits in the Nanfan loss caption and the Beaver Wars entry; (7) the French and Indian War and the American Revolution are not given as war entries because no page opened dates their start; the Sullivan Expedition and the 1780 raids are. Dates the pages dispute: Erie 1656 (Beaver Wars) or 1657 (Iroquois); Huron 1648 winter raid (Beaver Wars) or 1649 (Iroquois); Susquehannock 1675 or 1680. The Haldimand Tract (950,000 acres, 1784, Sullivan page) and the Six Nations of the Grand River have no land named on the pages: a gap-filler should open 'Haldimand Proclamation' and 'Six Nations of the Grand River'. Counterparts needing dossiers: wendat-confederacy (this group, written after this one), the Neutral Nation, Erie, Susquehannock, Petun, Wenro, Mannahoac, Mahican and Tuscarora (small peoples; no Cliopatria row); united-states-of-america, new-france and english/british colonial empires belong to am-07/am-06/eu-11 and should carry the mirror events (the 1701 gain by the English, the 1768 and 1784 gains by the Crown and the United States). Gazetteer: all names used resolve (Iroquoia, Ohio Country, places Orillia, Barrie, Hamilton, Cleveland, Erie, Harrisburg, Williamsport, Buffalo, Albany, rivers Niagara and Mohawk); the Petun, Mannahoac and Wenro lands are expressed as discs because the gazetteer lacks Huronia, Petun, Neutral and Erie country regions.
