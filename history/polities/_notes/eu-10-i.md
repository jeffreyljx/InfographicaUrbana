## principality-of-monaco

Merged rows: Cliopatria has two rows for Wikidata Q235, 'Principality of Monaco' (1642-2024, 5 shapes) and 'Kingdom of Monaco' (1815-1939, 4 shapes). They are one state: Monaco was a principality throughout (every page opened calls it one; none calls it a kingdom) and the rows alternate without overlap (Principality 1642-1814, Kingdom 1815-1939, Principality 1945-2024) and carry the same polygons (164.4 km2, then 94.1 km2). One dossier under the earlier slug, both Names in cliopatria_names, all nine shapes reviewed. Nobody should write a separate kingdom-of-monaco dossier (the eu-20 briefs list it as owned); colours.json holds a colour for each slug and the kingdom's is unused.

Start: 1297, the Grimaldi seizure of the Rock (Monaco page infobox 8 January 1297), not Cliopatria's 1642. New keyframes cover the Rock 1297-1300 and 1331-1345, plus Menton 1346-1354, all three places 1355-1356, 1395, 1397-1401 and 1419-1641, and Menton and Roquebrune alone 1357-1394, 1396 and 1402-1418 (the repair pass below explains the Genoese interruptions); nothing is drawn for 1301-1330. If the project prefers sovereign statehood, the start would be 1641 (Treaty of Péronne, 14 September 1641) or 1633 (Spain's recognition of Honoré II, Monaco page); the ledger and keyframes from 1642 stand either way.

Cliopatria geometry: every Monaco polygon is wrong in size and the post-1848 ones are wrong in place. 164.4 km2 (1645-1847) for about 40 km2 of land; 94.1 km2 (1848-2024) and 47.0 km2 (1936-1939) for 2.084 km2, with a bbox of 7.15-7.23E that does not contain Monaco (7.41E, gazetteer) and France as the only modern country. I marked the post-1848 shapes wrong and gave the named country. The grid is about 253 km2 per cell, so a 2 km2 state is a single cell at best: {country: Monaco} resolves to one cell centred about 7.29E 43.85N, while the Cliopatria 1945 shape resolves to a different cell near 7.2E 43.71N, and radii under about 15 km around Monaco resolve to no cell. The rendering and gazetteer agents need a rule for sub-cell polities; a dossier cannot fix that.

Gaps in Cliopatria that the dossier covers: 1297-1641 (new keyframes), 1794-1813 (right: France held Monaco; the ledger records the loss in 1793 and the gain in May 1814), 1940-1944 (new keyframe; the state continued, neutral, occupied from 11 November 1942).

Protectors and suzerains for the other dossiers (vassal_start/vassal_end belong to the suzerain, with 'principality-of-monaco' as counterpart): Genoese sovereignty from 1297 (Monaco page infobox); Spanish authority and garrison from 1524, 'an autonomous state for about a century' (History of Monaco; the page names the Treaty of Burgos and, inconsistently, the treaty of Tordesilla), Honoré II recognised by Spain in 1633 (Monaco page); French protection from the Treaty of Péronne, 14 September 1641 (500 French soldiers paid by the French crown under the prince's command, rent of 75,000 livres a year); Sardinian protectorate from the Congress of Vienna, 1815, confirmed by the Treaty of Stupinigi in 1817, ending in 1860 (Monaco page: 'Monaco remained in this position until 1860'); limited French protection from the July 1918 treaty, endorsed in 1919 (Monaco page); the 2002 treaty (Monaco independent if the dynasty ends). These are not in this ledger because the schema puts a vassal relation in the suzerain's ledger. The eu-13 (Sardinia, Genoa, Savoy) and France dossiers should carry them.

Disagreements between pages: (1) Sardinian protection of the free cities: History of Monaco says 1849, the Menton page 'two years later' than 1848 (1850), and Roquebrune says two years of independence then ten years of Savoy administration. (2) The formal 1861 act: Menton page 2 February 1861, Monaco infobox 11 February 1861 (Franco-Monegasque Treaty); both are February, the ledger uses month 2. (3) Roquebrune's 1861 'plebiscite' versus the Menton page's plebiscite of 15-16 April 1860 and payment in 1861. (4) Italian occupation: Monaco page 'In 1943', History of Monaco 11 November 1942; the ledger follows the dated sentence. (5) The Monaco page says the annexation of Menton and Roquebrune 'by almost ten times' increased the territory, History of Monaco says the 1861 loss was 'about 95%' (a twentyfold ratio); the keyframe notes use 95%.

Seller of Menton (1346) and Roquebrune (1355): the pages are silent, so the gain counterparts are plain strings, not slugs. 1645: no page explains Cliopatria's 141 to 164.4 km2 step; treated as a polygon artefact and corrected from the Treaty of Péronne, which names all three places in 1641.

Free cities of Menton and Roquebrune (1848-1860): a small entity with no Cliopatria row; the loss counterpart is its descriptive name. It may deserve a stub dossier (suggested slug free-cities-of-menton-and-roquebrune) or none; it was under Sardinian protection and administration, and France annexed it in 1860-1861.

Not collected: the 1911 constitution (5 January 1911, Monaco infobox) and the 1962 and 2002 acts, which move no border; Treaty of Burgos 1524 article; the French Revolutionary Wars article (the war name in wars[] is descriptive).

Pages: eight distinct pages opened in all after the repair pass (the original six plus List of rulers of Monaco and Charles I, Lord of Monaco), against a budget of five; in the original run six opened against a budget of five (the merged Kingdom row adds four shapes and the extension to 1297 adds three keyframes; the Franco-Monegasque Treaty page proved a stub). Where I disagree with the reviewer, and why:
- The reviewer's recipe says to correct keyframe 0 to 'Genoa, then Aragon from the late 1300s to 1419'. I did not. The Monaco page does say 'in the late 1300s Genoa lost Monaco after fighting the Crown of Aragon over Corsica' and 'In 1419, the Grimaldi family purchased Monaco from the Crown of Aragon', but the List of rulers of Monaco dates three further Genoese spans inside that window (1395-12 to 1397-05, 1402-11 to 1419-06) and says the sons of Rainier II 'bought back Monaco from Genoa'. History of Monaco's medieval section does not mention Aragon at all, and the Charles I page does not either. The ledger follows the list page (counterpart republic-of-genoa for the 1419 gain) and the Monaco page's Aragonese interlude is recorded as a disagreement in the dossier notes and in the 1419 event's note; the Monaco page's 'late 1300s' loss by Genoa may be the January 1395 regain.
- The reviewer quotes the Monaco page as saying 'the struggle over "the Rock" continued for another century'. The fetch of the page returned 'Francesco was evicted a few years later by the Genoese forces' but not that continuation, so the dossier quotes only the first sentence.
- The Rainier II 'yielded for 20,000 fl.' sentence is undated on the list page; the reviewer ties it to 1357. I tie the Genoese control line to 1357 at confidence 2 and say the yielding is undated.

Also seen on the list page and not put in the ledger: Jean I 'gained sovereignty for Monaco' on 20 November 1441 (possible end of the Genoese sovereignty named in the 1297 infobox; no page opened says so); the 1793-1814 French rule dated 19 January 1793 (National Convention) and 24 February 1793 (annexation) as the fetch summarised the table, which would give the 1793 loss a month; Honoré IV from 30 May 1814 against the infobox's 17 May 1814 restoration.
