## new-france

SCOPE. Cliopatria's 'New France' row (1609-1791) is not New France as the Wikipedia article defines it ('five colonies' in 1712: Canada, Acadia, Hudson Bay, Terre-Neuve, Louisiana). Counting H3 cells, about one quarter of every shape is French colonies elsewhere: Maranhao in Brazil (1612-1625), Saint Martin (1626-1631), Guadeloupe and Martinique (1636-), Fort-Dauphin in Madagascar (1645-1672), Grenada (1653-), Saint-Louis in Senegal (1659-), Saint-Domingue and Cayenne (1670-), Reunion (1670-1672), the Ivory Coast (1691-), the Seychelles (1757-1761) and Guyana (1783-1790). This dossier keeps only the Canadian and United States cells (verdict adjust, correction 'intersect with Canada and the United States'). Saint-Domingue and French Guiana already have dossiers (saint-domingue 1659-1791, french-colony-of-guiana 1643-1946, both written by am-06-x); every other overseas piece has none and will vanish from the map unless a gap-filler gives it one: French West Indies (Guadeloupe, Martinique, Saint Martin, Grenada, the Grenadines), French Senegal, Fort-Dauphin and Reunion, the Ivory Coast posts, France Equinoxiale (Maranhao), the Seychelles, and Saint Pierre and Miquelon (the only North American land France kept in 1763; Cliopatria draws it as the 1791 shape of this row and again in 1805-1814 as 'First French colonial empire').

START AND END. Cliopatria starts in 1609 and ends in 1791. The dossier starts in 1604 (Saint Croix Island) and ends on 10 February 1763 (Treaty of Paris, 'Ceded to Britain: 10 February 1763'); the 1763-1791 shapes are marked wrong because they are Spanish Louisiana (spanish-louisiana, from November 1762), Saint-Domingue, Guiana and the other overseas pieces. New France is empty (no land) in 1629-1631 (English held Quebec, Acadia and Cape Breton) and the keyframes use 'a region minus itself' for an empty shape.

PAGE DISAGREEMENTS. (1) Utrecht: the New France page dates it 1713 in places and 1715 in others; the Treaty of Utrecht page, the Acadia page and the Ile-Royale page say 1713. (2) Louisiana's capital moved to New Orleans in 1722 on the infobox and in the body and in 1723 in the timeline; the dossier does not use it. (3) Cape Breton in 1629: the treaty page says the English 'had seized' Cape Breton in 1629; the Cape Breton page has French settlements at Englishtown (1629) and St. Peter's (1630) and a Scottish colony at Baleine that the French 'quickly defeated'; the Acadia page dates English control of Acadia from 1621. The ledger follows the treaty page (loss 1629, gain 1632). (4) Cahokia: 1696 on the Illinois Country page, 1699 in Cliopatria's usual story; the page is followed. (5) Placentia: 'made Placentia (Plaisance) their capital' in 1655 and 'built Fort Plaisance in 1662' on the same page; 1662 is used. (6) The Illinois Country page gives Marquette's mission as 1675.

## new-sweden

Cliopatria's New Sweden is 1640-1658 (two identical 2,841 km2 shapes); the colony lasted 1638 (landing 29 March) to 15 September 1655, so a core keyframe is added for 1638-1639 and the 1653 shape is cut at 1655 (1656-1658 are New Netherland, whose dossier new-netherland already holds the matching Fort Casimir and Fort Christina events). Neither page gives an area. The map outline is one block along both banks; the east bank only becomes Swedish with Fort Elfsborg in 1643 (ledger). Month of the founding is the landing date, 29 March 1638; the Fort Christina page does not give the construction month. Coordinates for the capital and the Fort Casimir radius are the editor's. Pages opened: 2 of a budget of 1-2. The Lenape (counterpart 'nobody' in the 1638 and 1643 gains) have no Cliopatria row; if am-05 writes a Lenape dossier the counterpart can be changed.
