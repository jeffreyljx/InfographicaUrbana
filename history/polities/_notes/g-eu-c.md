## transcaucasian-democratic-federative-republic (1918-04 .. 1918-05)
- No Cliopatria row. One new keyframe: the union of the three successors' Cliopatria 1918 shapes plus Kars, less a 45 km disc at Batumi (Ottoman-occupied 14 April) and a 40 km disc at Baku (Baku Commune 13 April-25 July). The discs are my radii; the TDFR page gives no area or boundary.
- The shape overlaps the 1918 keyframes of georgia, armenia and azerbaijan-democratic-republic by construction (it is their union); the successors' found events (May 1918) take the land over, my split events hand it on. The compiler should let the successors win at the 1918 snap.
- Baku: the Azerbaijan Democratic Republic's Cliopatria 1918 shape includes Baku, which the Baku Commune held until July 1918. Needs a dossier: baku-commune (1918-04-13 .. 1918-07-25/26). The Baku Commune page does not mention the Seim or the federation.
- Transcaucasian Commissariat (15 Nov 1917) and Seim (23 Feb 1918) are the federation's earlier government, not a separate state; Cliopatria draws their land as Russian Republic until 22 April 1918.
- Disagreement: TDFR page has Armenia and Azerbaijan declaring independence on 28 May 1918; the Caucasus campaign page has 'the same day' as Georgia (26 May). The armenia and azerbaijan-democratic-republic dossiers use 28 May.
- Kars: held by Armenian forces until 25 April (TDFR page); the Caucasus campaign page has the siege from 23 April, surrender agreed 25 April. Ardahan is undated on both pages, so it is in the contest zone only.
- Native names, wikidata, rulers (Chkheidze, Chkhenkeli have no reign dates) omitted.

## principality-of-rostov (1219 .. 1474)
- No Cliopatria row and no Wikipedia article ('Principality of Rostov' redirects to the Vladimir-Suzdal article, which names Rostov only among the appanages). The state rests on the Vasilko Konstantinovich page (Prince of Rostov 1219-1238), Konstantin of Rostov (1207, died 2 Feb 1218), the town page and the Ivan III annexation list ('Rostov in 1474').
- Start year: Vasilko 1219 (page). Alternatives on the pages: 1207 (Konstantin sent to rule Rostov), 1216 (Konstantin 'divided Vladimir-Suzdal ... creating three new appanage principalities'), 1218 (Yaroslavl's start in its dossier).
- Extent: none on any page. The shape is the 40 km disc at Rostov Veliky that the Vladimir-Suzdal, Yaroslavl and Moscow dossiers already use. Cliopatria draws the land inside Rostov-Suzdal (to 1240), the Mongol Empire (1241-1293) and Vladimir-Suzdal (from 1294); the Vladimir-Suzdal dossier hands it over only in 1389 (its ledger 9, confidence 1), repeated here as the 1389 gain.
- Not on any page opened: princes after Vasilko (Boris, Gleb have no dates), the extent, any purchase by Moscow before 1474. No ruler strip.

## principality-of-uglich (1218 .. 1482)
- No Cliopatria row, no article of its own (the Uglich article is the town's). Pages: independent princedom 1218-1328, 'the local princes sold their rights to the great prince of Moscow'; afterwards held by princes of Moscow's house (Konstantin Dmitriyevich, 'prince of Uglich and Pskov', 1389-1433; Andrey Bolshoy from 1462); Bezhetsk annexed by Moscow in the early 15th century, own prince from 1433 'subordinate to the Grand Prince of Moscow'.
- The Vladimir-Suzdal dossier (1389) asked this dossier to decide 'state or part of Moscow'. Kept as a state 1218-1482 so that the three dossiers agree: VS loses the Uglich-Bezhetsk lands to it in 1389 (ledger 10), Moscow gains Bezhetsk from it in 1415 (ledger 20) and the Uglich country in 1482 (ledger 35), all at confidence 1 and all Cliopatria-shape readings. The pages put these lands in Moscow's hands from 1328. A gap-filler should decide whether those three events should name grand-principality-of-moscow or nobody, and then end this dossier in 1328 (and the 1462 Andrey Bolshoy appanage as Moscow's own).
- End year: Ivan III page, 'In 1491, Andrey Bolshoy was arrested ... He died in prison in 1493, and Ivan seized his land.' The dossier ends 1482, Cliopatria's year in the Moscow dossier; the difference is flagged in the dissolve event.
- The 1328 sale and the 1462 grant are `rename` events (change of line within one state); no land moves at either.
- Ivan I's purchase of Uglich (cited from the Moscow page by the Vladimir-Suzdal dossier) is not on the Donskoy page; Vladimir Konstantinovich (first prince, Konstantin page infobox) has no dates on any page opened.

- No Cliopatria row; two new keyframes on a 120 km disc round Seiyun (the Tahirid page: 'controlled much of the interior of Hadramawt'; no area or boundary anywhere), less a 10 km disc at Shibam from 1858. Soft. A gap-filler with a map source should replace the disc.

- Gazetteer: 'Tarim' (Yemen) has no place entry; the disc round Seiyun covers it.

## syrian-city-states (NOT WRITTEN)
- Not a state: a collective name for the Late Bronze Age kingdoms of Syria, each with its own king. Thutmose III page: the page does not present them as one polity; it has separate cities (Ardata and Simyra, year 29-30; Ullaza, year 31; Aleppo and Carchemish unconquered; Nuhasse, years 34 and 38; Tunip; Kadesh) and 'princes', 'kings' and 'nobles'. Suppiluliuma I page: 'kingdoms' and 'petty kingdoms' (Nuhashshe, Kadesh, Aleppo, Mukish/Alalakh, Niya, Arahtu, Qatna, Amurru, Ugarit, Carchemish), each with its own ruler, previously under Mittanian suzerainty. No common government, so no borders were drawn.
- What the citing dossiers should use instead: new-kingdom-of-egypt ledger 6 and 10 (vassal_start -1447, vassal_end -1352) and hittites ledger 5 (gain -1345) should name the individual kingdoms. Dossiers that exist: qatna (Egyptian vassal -1446 to -1337, Hittite loss -1337), amurru, ugarit, mitanni (Carchemish). Dossiers missing: Kadesh, Nuhashshe, Mukish (Alalakh), Niya, Arahtu, Tunip; until then use `nobody` for the stateless remainder. Dates in the pages: Simyra taken by Thutmose III (sixth campaign, year 30), Nuhashshe raided year 34 and 38; no Hittite date on the Suppiluliuma I page (the Mitanni page gives 1345).
