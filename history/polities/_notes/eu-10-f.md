## Read these first

1. **county-of-touraine: Cliopatria's row is La Marche, and the state is now only the apanage years (repaired 2026-10-25).** The eight shapes of the row "County of Touraine" (1206-1304, 1363-1491; 43-47 gazetteer cells) sit on Creuse (24/24 cells) and Haute-Vienne (13/24), i.e. the County of La Marche and the Limousin, 150 km south-east of Tours; none shares a cell with the region Touraine (Indre-et-Loire, 26 cells). All eight are `wrong` with an empty correction. The dossier now draws Touraine only while an apanage holder had it (1360-1362 Philip the Bold, 1386-1391 Louis of Orléans, 1417-1422 Charles, merged into the crown on 21 October 1422); the royal duchy of 1205-1359 stays in kingdom-of-france. **A dossier for the County of La Marche is needed** (suggested slug `county-of-la-marche`, 1206-1491; its keyframes can be `{"cliopatria": "County of Touraine", "year": y}`); the house-of-bourbon dossier already has La Marche events (1327, 1341, 1488). The orchestrator decides whether this Cliopatria row becomes that dossier; until then the La Marche land has no state.
2. **county-of-poitou ends in 1271, not 1249.** Alphonse of Poitiers is Count of Poitiers 1225-1271 (Alphonse page infobox) and Poitou 'reverted to the royal domain' on his death in 1271 (County of Auvergne page). The kingdom-of-france dossier has a 1250 gain of Poitou from this slug and a 1271 gain from 'Alfonso, Count of Poitou (apanage)': one of the two should be dropped, and the rendering agent must resolve the overlap with the France leaf shape in 1250-1271. Cliopatria's Poitou polygon is also in the wrong place: it holds all of Charente (the Angoulême the County of Poitou page lists as a neighbouring county) and only about half of Deux-Sèvres and Vienne; all shapes are adjusted to the region Poitou (Vendée, Deux-Sèvres, Vienne, 81 cells).
3. **county-of-auvergne: the county is the Vic-le-Comte remnant plus Boulogne, not the Duchy of Auvergne (repaired 2026-10-25).** The Auvergne page says Philip Augustus 'linked the majority of the county to the royal territory' and 'the Count held out in Vic-le-Comte'; the Duchy page dates the Terre royale to 1213, the occupation of Riom to 1195, and says the 1356/1360 duchy for John of Berry existed 'while the County of Auvergne continued to exist in parallel'. The shapes 1206-1333 and 1352-1501 are adjusted to a 30 km radius round Vic-le-Comte (the radius is mine; no page gives a boundary) plus Boulogne from 1260 (Lauragais from 1477); the royal land goes to kingdom-of-france in 1213 and is no longer folded back in 1360 or handed to house-of-bourbon in 1416: it is the Duchy of Berry and Auvergne, which needs its own dossier. The state now ends in 1524 (Anne, sovereign Countess 1501-1524), not 1501. A gap-filler should open the French 'Terre d'Auvergne' pages for the boundary.
4. **county-of-perigord shapes 1206-1401 are adjusted to the Dordogne**: they hold only 22-23 of its 38 cells and spill over Lot-et-Garonne (Agenais) and the Gironde; the Brétigny list names Agenais, Périgord, Limousin and Quercy as separate territories. The 1440-1481 polygon (Dordogne plus Corrèze and Haute-Vienne) is kept unverified; the county-of-foix dossier (House of Albret page) says the Albrets inherited the County of Périgord and the Viscounty of Limoges together in 1470.
