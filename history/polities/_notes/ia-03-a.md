## kazakh-khanate

Phase B dossier for ia-03-a, written 2026-10-19 from 14 opened Wikipedia pages (the page budget for 43 shapes is 14); one more page, Lesser Juz (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lesser_J%C3%BCz), did not load (url_not_found, retried once) and is not a source. WHAT THE PAGES COULD NOT SETTLE
1. Infobox area: the Kazakh Khanate infobox gives none, so Cliopatria's areas (peak 2.65 M km2 in 1595-1608; 2.40 M in 1521-1571; 1.71 M in 1822-1847) are uncompared; the page gives only a population that 'reached 1 million people' under Kasym.
2. Founding: the page dates the khanate 1465 (Kazakh historiography) or 1470-1471 (Russian); Cliopatria's first shape is 1468; the Kerei page gives no founding year (late 1450s breakaway). The dossier starts in 1465 and adds a new keyframe for 1465-1467.
3. Cliopatria's 1475-1501 shapes contain the Timurid and Shaybanid heartland (Samarkand, Bukhara, Kashkadarya, Surkhandarya, Khatlon, Tashkent: 1,925 cells taken from the Timurid shape in 1475). No opened page supports it and two contradict it (Moghulistan: Tashkent taken from the Timurids in 1482 or 1484, lost to Shaybani in 1503; Muhammad Shaybani: Samarkand from the Timurids in 1500). Verdict adjust for 1475-1497 with the Uzbek, Tajik and Turkmen part removed (1,196 cells). The 729 cells in Kazakhstan are kept and gained in 1475 from the Uzbek heirs of Abu'l-Khayr (repair round, below). The Timurid and Shaybanid dossiers (ia-02, ia-03) should check that no cell is claimed twice (270 are, with timurid-empire).
4. The Syr Darya in 1510: the Kasym Khan page has the Uzbek Khanate agree to give 'all former Uzbek lands north of the Syr Darya' without a year; Cliopatria adds them in 1521 (956 cells from its Bukhara shape). Dated 1510 at confidence 1; the 1512-1520 shapes are adjusted to include South Kazakhstan and Qyzylorda.
5. Tashkent: the Kazakh page has it Kazakh at Kasym's death, 'between 1598 and 1723' (Tashkent page), and as a second capital 1729-1781, but also Shaybanid in the 16th century, ruled by Keldi Muhammad 1598-1604, under the Dzungars 1723-1745, taken for a time by Irdana Biy of Kokand in 1765, independent under Yunus Khoja 1784-1807 and annexed by Kokand in 1809. A 'Tashkent state (Yunus Khoja), 1784-1809' dossier is needed (counterpart in the 1784 loss).
6. No land is named, so shapes 1529 and 1556 are ok with 'unverified: page budget' and a contest over Semirechye 1533-1565 is recorded; the Haqnazar Khan page and Muhammad Haidar Dughlat's Tarikh-i Rashidi are the places to look.

8. The Khiva loss of 1791 (725 cells) and the Kokand losses of 1809 and 1822 (259 and 396 cells) are Cliopatria's; only the Tashkent annexation of 1809 and 'the 1820s' are on pages. Pages to open: Khanate of Khiva, Senior Juz, Aral Sea fortresses (Kokand), Kokand Syr Darya line.
9. The Russian oaths: 10 October 1731 (Junior Juz, Abu'l Khayr to Empress Anna), shortly after by Semeke of the Middle Juz; the Jüz page dates the Middle jüz's incorporation 1734-1740 and the Abylai page his oath at Orenburg 1740; a footnote (Bregel): the oaths were made 'between 1731 and 1740' and stayed purely nominal until the end of the 18th century. These are Russian suzerainty over Kazakh lands: they belong to the Russian Empire dossier (vassal_start, counterpart kazakh-khanate, Junior Juz 1731, Middle Juz 1740) and not to this ledger, which would put the khanate's own vassals. Likewise the Dzungar overlordship of the Greater and Middle Juz (1734-1735 and 1740-1742) and of the Junior Juz (1742) belongs to the Dzungar dossier, and the Qing suzerainty over Abylai (1757) to qing-dynasty.
10. The three jüzes: after Tauke (1715 or 1718) the zhuzes 'essentially became independent khanates' and the first khans of the three zhuzes were established in 1721 (Juz page); Abylai ruled all three from 1771 to 1781, Vali the northern Middle Juz after him. Cliopatria keeps one Kazakh shape; this dossier follows it and records the Russian takeover of the Middle Juz (1822) and Junior Juz (1824) as hatched occupation, ending in 1837 (Kenesary's revolt, contest) and 1847.
11. 1846-1847: the Kenesary page has him killed by Kyrgyz manaps and calls him the last khan; the Kazakh page has him surrender in 1846 and die fighting Kokand forces. The khanate's end year 1847 is Cliopatria's and the infobox's.
12. Rulers: the page's table (copied) has gaps, 1715-1771 and 1781-1841, and overlapping claimants in 1533-1538; Kerei's reign is given as 1456-1473 and 1465-1480 on the same page. The strip is a record, not a succession.
13. Capitals: Sozaq, Syghanaq and Saraishyq are not in the gazetteer; the coordinates are approximate (listed in each capital's note). The infobox capital dates (Saraishyq 1511-1521, Turkistan 1599-1781, Tashkent 1729-1781) are taken as given.
14. Counterpart rows: Cliopatria's Oirat Confederation (1619-1676) and Dzungar Khanate (1609-1618, 1677-1756) rows are the Dzungars of the page; 1643 and 1658 use oirat-confederation, 1684 and later dzungar-khanate. Cliopatria's Western Moghulistan is the Semirechye part of Moghulistan.

NOT CHANGED, and why (finding 8, vassal events). I did not add vassal_start/vassal_end pairs for the Russian oaths (Junior jüz 1731, Middle jüz 1740), the Dzungar overlordship (1734-1742) or the Qing (1757). The schema (history/briefs/dossier.schema.json, ledger type vassal_start) reads: 'region becomes or ceases to be this state's vassal ... counterpart is the vassal itself in both events'. A vassal_start in this ledger would make the Kazakh Khanate the suzerain and russian-empire, dzungar-khanate or qing-dynasty its vassal, which is the wrong way round, and the map would tint Russian land as a Kazakh client. The overlord's dossier carries the event with counterpart kazakh-khanate (the pages already in sources: Kazakh Khanate and Jüz pages for 1731 and 1734-1740, Abylai Khan page for the 1740 oath at Orenburg and the 1757 Qing suzerainty, Dzungar Khanate page for 1734-1745). If the orchestrator rules the reverse, the pairs are quick to add. Occupation events are different: the schema has the owner record them with the roles reversed, which this ledger already does (Dzungars 1723-1745, Russia 1822-1847).
