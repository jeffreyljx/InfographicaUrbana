## senussi-order (1843..Jan 1932, state, 7 new keyframes, 18 events)
- Pages: Senussi, Second Italo-Senussi War, Pacification of Libya (returned the same text as the Second Italo-Senussi War; probably one article), Italo-Turkish War, Kufra, Jaghbub.
- No page gives the order's territory or an infobox area. Shapes: discs round the seats (Bayda 1843, Jaghbub 1855, Kufra's Jawf 1894), then the gazetteer's Cyrenaica minus the Italian enclaves (1912), minus the northern lowlands (1924: Benghazi and Marj districts as stand-in), minus Jaghbub (1926), minus Kufra (1931).
- Contest pairs follow the citing dossiers: 1912-1917 against italian-africa (region copied from italian-africa ledger 16/19, including Tripolitania, which no page supports as Senussi land: the Italo-Turkish page only says the order 'controlled most of Libya'; a renderer may restrict it to Cyrenaica); 1923-1932 and the 1932 loss recorded against both italian-libya and italian-africa because both cite senussi-order.
- italian-libya ledger 13 and italian-africa ledger 38 record Kufra 1931 as a gain from nobody; this dossier records it as a Senussi loss. Egypt's cession of Jaghbub/Kufra in 1925 (italian-africa ledger 32) is on paper; the Senussi lost Jaghbub in early 1926 (Jaghbub page).
- Accord of al-Rajma (October 1920, Idris Emir of Cyrenaica, autonomous administration of Kufra, Jalu, Jaghbub, Awjila, Ajdabiya) has no territorial event; it is in the 1917 event note.
- Page-silent: the Senussi-French conflict in Chad and Wadai (undated on the Senussi page), Borkou and Tibesti, the Fezzan; when the Awjila-Ajdabiya oases fell; the day of Kufra's fall. The Jaghbub summary mentions a Senussi return to Jaghbub in 1928, contradicting its own 1926 occupation; not used.
- The Emirate of Cyrenaica (1949-1951) is another dossier; Idris became king in 1951.

## british-south-africa-company (1890 Oct..1924 Apr, colony, 6 new keyframes, 14 events)
- Pages: British South Africa Company, First Matabele War, Barotseland, Northern Rhodesia, Southern Rhodesia.
- Start 1890 (first Administrator, Pioneer Column at Fort Salisbury), not the charter's 20 December 1889: no land before 1890.
- Gains copy the citing dossiers' regions: Matabeleland and Midlands from ndebele-kingdom (ledger 9-12), the Lozi outer realm from lozi-kingdom (ledger 5). 1900: Zambia without Western Province and without the Lozi outer realm (as british-africa ledger 8 does); 1911: Western Province (Bulozi).
- Vassal pairs with united-kingdom (Mashonaland 1891, Northern Rhodesia 1900 to 1923 and 1924) follow the brief's colony rule; the united-kingdom dossier has no matching BSAC events and should get them. british-africa ledger 3, 6, 8 draw the same Rhodesian land as British Africa's; once compiled the two overlap.
- Counterparts without dossiers: southern-rhodesia (annexed 12 Sept 1923) and northern-rhodesia (1 April 1924); Cliopatria draws both inside British Africa.
- Page conflicts: the 9 May 1891 Order-in-Council is 'a protectorate initially covering Mashonaland' on the BSAC page and 'the area of what became Northern Rhodesia ... placed under BSAC administration' on the Northern Rhodesia page; no administrator reached Barotseland before 1895. The Matabeleland Order-in-Council of 18 July 1894 is not on the First Matabele War page.
- Not dated on pages opened: treaties with chiefs, Katanga, Manica boundary, Nyasaland.

## tunjur-kingdom (1450..1635, state, 2 new keyframes, 5 events)
- Pages: Wadai Empire, Sultanate of Darfur, Tunjur people, Ain Farah. No dedicated Tunjur kingdom article exists.
- Start 1450 is a confidence-1 bridge: the Wadai page has the kingdom 'established around the 15th century', the Tunjur people page has Tunjur rule in Darfur and Wadai from the 13th century.
- Extent: Darfur region plus the Wadai heartland (the wadai-empire dossier's own region) until the mid-16th century; then the Wadai heartland alone until 1635. Central Darfur goes to sultanate-of-darfur (1550, its rounding of 'mid-16th century'); the rest of Darfur to nobody (page-silent; sultanate-of-darfur ledger 1 gains it from nobody in 1609).
- Open question: one Tunjur state or two (Darfur and Wadai)? The Darfur page makes Musa, son of the Darfur founder Ahmad al-Ma'qur, the subjugator of Wadai.
- Capital: Ain Farah placed by 'about 130 km northwest of El Fasher' (the page has no coordinates); no seat named for 1551-1635. Daju (earlier rulers per the Tunjur people page) are a candidate dossier.

## beylik-of-constantine (1830..1837, state, 3 new keyframes, 6 events)
- Pages: Beylik of Constantine, French conquest of Algeria, Annaba.
- Drawn from 1830 (fall of Algiers; Ahmed Bey 'invested'), not from the infobox's 1528: until 1830 it is a province under the Dey, drawn inside the Ottoman and Regency rows; regency-of-algiers lists it as successor. Extent: Constantinois plus Béjaïa and Biskra provinces (89,800 km2) against the page's 430 x 330 km (about 142,000 km2 as a rectangle), ratio 0.63.
- Losses mirror french-algeria: Annaba 1832 (30 km), Bougie 1833 (35 km; french-algeria ledger 2 says nobody, the page names Kabyle and Kouloughli defenders), Constantine 1837 (40 km, day not on the pages opened; 13 October is french-algeria ledger 8's reading). The rest of the province is nobody's from 1837 (french-algeria ledger 13).
- Ahmed Bey's holdout in the Aurès until 1848 is not drawn: no page gives land. The Annaba page does not mention the Beylik.
