## What the Cliopatria rows get wrong (decisions)

- **later-liang-dynasty** (911-925): ended 19 November 923, not 925. The outline (793,636 km2) keeps the north bank of the Yellow River until 921; the Zhu Youzhen page has Tianxiong go over to Jin in 915 and 'nearly all of the Later Liang territory north of the Yellow River' fall to Jin by 916, so keyframes 916 and 922 cut Hebei, Shanxi, Beijing and Tianjin. The row shares Wikidata Q783489 with Cliopatria's Liang Dynasty (502-557), which the existing `liang-dynasty` dossier already uses; they are different states, not merged, and this dossier's wikidata is null. The Di-led Later Liang of 386-403 is the separate `later-liang`.
- **later-jin** (911-921 and 936-946): two unrelated shapes. The 911-921 shape (Shanxi, 71,612 km2) is the Jin of Li Keyong and Li Cunxu, the Later Tang's precursor, verdict wrong here; the former-jin dossier (another batch) should take 911-921 as well as its 922-925 row. Shi Jingtang's state runs 28 November 936 - 11 January 947, not 936-946. Its 936 year is Hedong only (new keyframe 936b); the Later Tang heartland comes on Li Congke's fall (11 January 937 per the Later Tang page).
- **later-jin / Sixteen Prefectures**: the pages disagree on the cession (promised 936 in the Five Dynasties page; 937 once and 938 in the Sixteen Prefectures page; undated in the Later Jin and Later Tang pages). The dossier gives it 938 at confidence 2 and keeps the land in a 937 keyframe, but Cliopatria's Liao shape holds it from 936: the Liao dossier (ea-05) must agree. The Later Jin was also a Liao tributary 936-943 ('promised annual tribute'): a vassal_start/vassal_end pair for later-jin belongs in the Liao dossier.
- **qi-kingdom** (911-925): Cliopatria's shape (Pingliang, southern Ningxia, eastern Gansu; 94,436 km2) does not contain Fengxiang, the capital every page names: that country sits wholly inside Cliopatria's Former Shu shape from 911. The dossier adds Fengxiang and Qin Prefecture (Tianxiong) until 915, and from 916 draws only 'Fengxiang Municipality and its immediate surroundings' (Li Maozhen page), recording the Pingliang-Ningxia part as stateless at confidence 1. Qi ended in 924 (Li Maozhen submitted that spring), not 925.
- **former-shu** (911-925): the two shapes are the same 605,241 km2 outline and contain Baoji and 69 percent of the Tianshui country from 911, but the Wang Jian page dates Qin and Feng prefectures to autumn 915 and Baoji to autumn 916. Five keyframes (907, 911, 915, 916, 922) cut them back. Guizhou and Yunnan fringes are unverified and left. The state starts 3 November 907 and ends 925 (no month on any page).
- **min** (911-946): ended 2 October 945; Cliopatria's 946 year belongs to the Southern Tang (Jian, Ting), Wuyue (Fuzhou, vassal from winter 946-spring 947) and Liu Congxiao's Qingyuan (Quan, Zhang). Starts 27 April 909. A Yin secession (943-945) is a separate rebel state, written.
- **southern-chu** (911-959): ended December 951 (Ma Xi'e deposed; the Southern Tang held Tanzhou until 1 December 952), not 959; 952-963 is the Wuping Jiedushi. The outline (416,709 km2) is cut to Hunan, the Guilin country and the Wuzhou country (about 296,000 km2, ratio 0.71) because both the Ma Chu and Ma Yin pages say 'present-day Hunan and northeastern Guangxi'; Guizhou, Hubei, Jiangxi and Guangdong fringes have no page. The kingdom starts 907 (Prince of Chu); the Ma Chu page also gives 927 for 'the establishment of the Kingdom'.
- Infobox areas: none of the pages gives one, so no ratio was possible except the corrected-Chu comparison and Fujian (Min shape 121,944 km2 is 98 percent of modern Fujian).
