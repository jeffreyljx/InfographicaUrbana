## republic-of-pisa (Cliopatria 1010..1401, 8 shapes)

- Dates changed against Cliopatria: the state starts about 1000 (infobox 'Established: c. 1000') and ends 1406 (Florence's annexation), not 1401. The Visconti purchase of 13 February 1399 is a rename event, not an end.
- The islands run on different clocks than Cliopatria's shapes: Corsica gained 1051 (Pisa page; Cliopatria 1085, Corsica page has Pisan administration from 1090), lost 1284 after Meloria (Corsica page; the Pisa page says 1295; Cliopatria 1394). Sardinia is dominion from 1207 (Pisa page) to 1324 (Pisa and Kingdom of Sardinia pages), not 1188 to 1394. The 1188 and 1202 shapes therefore remove the Sardinian cells; a new 1207 keyframe draws the Cagliari and Gallura lands as dominion. Cliopatria's 1395 shape (the contado alone) is correct from 1324.
- Disagreement with other dossiers: giudicato-of-arborea has the fall of Castel di Castro in June 1326, the pages opened here end Pisan Sardinia in 1324; giudicato-of-gallura has Gallura passing to Pisa in 1287, no page opened here says so (the vassal event runs 1207 to 1324 on the Pisa page's 'dominion over the giudicati'). The Cagliari page makes the Della Gherardesca family one of three heirs of Cagliari in 1258, so a whole-judicate shape overstates the direct holding.
- Not settled (page silent): the 1299 sale of part of Sardinia to Genoa, the minor islands (Elba, Gorgona, Pianosa, Giglio, Giannutri), the Balearic span 1115-1184 (infobox control span, drawn as occupation at confidence 2), the wars with Lucca, Florence and Volterra, Ugolino della Gherardesca, the destruction of Porto Pisano, Genoese Bonifacio of 1195 (the three southern Corsican cells that Cliopatria drops in 1202 are a loss at confidence 1: neither page explains them).

- Counterparts without a dossier: republic-of-genoa (Cliopatria row, eu-13, spans 785 years and probably needs splitting); Corsica's local rulers before 1051; the Balearic holders.

## norman-italy (Cliopatria 1030..1138, 9 shapes)

- The Cliopatria row is named for a process (Wikipedia title 'Norman conquest of southern Italy'). The dossier treats it as the Hauteville and Drengot conquest state: County of Aversa 1030, County of Apulia 1043, Duchy of Apulia and Calabria 1059, County of Sicily 1061, uniting under Roger II in 1127 and ending on his coronation in December 1130 (a hand-over: kingdom-of-sicily takes 1130 onward). Cliopatria's row runs to 1138 and the Kingdom row restarts in 1139; the kingdom dossier fills 1130-1138 with new keyframes.
- Corrections that other dossiers must follow: (1) Principality of Capua: the Aversa page makes the Drengot counts princes of Capua from 1058 and Roger II's page has the principality alive in 1134-1138, yet Cliopatria's row stops in 1065 and Norman Italy swallows the Caserta block from 1085 (removed here). (2) Principality of Salerno: pages put the fall in 1076-77; Cliopatria's row runs to 1084. (3) Duchy of Benevento (Cliopatria 12,234 km2) lies almost wholly inside Norman Italy's shapes of 1046-1055 and from 1085; the pages say only that Guiscard held the city 1078-1081; flagged for the eu-13 Benevento dossier. (4) Emirate of Sicily should end its island rows in 1091 (Noto), not 1093. (5) Bari is removed from Norman Italy until 1071 (the Byzantine city).
- Page disagreements: the Norman conquest page puts the recognition of William of Apulia at the Melfi council of September 1042, the Duchy page founds the county in 1043; the Norman conquest page omits the 1059 investiture of Guiscard (Duchy page: 1059); the Norman conquest page is internally inconsistent about Drogo (assassinated 1051, conquest of Benevento 1053).
- Not on any page opened: Cerami 1063, Otranto 1068, Syracuse 1085, Agrigento 1087, the terms of the Treaty of Mignano and of Honorius's Benevento treaty of 1128, William Iron Arm's reign years. Vassalage (counts under Guaimar of Salerno 1042, Guiscard a papal vassal from 1059, Roger I under the Duke of Apulia) is not drawn as events here; the suzerain's dossier carries them.
- Gazetteer names used only in text or reached through Cliopatria's 1034 shape: Aversa, Melfi, Mdina. The grid cell is about 250 km2, so Aversa and Capua are one to three cells each.

## kingdom-of-sicily (Cliopatria 1139..1384, 8 shapes)

- Treated as one state from 25 December 1130 to 1410 (the Kingdom page's infobox end): the unified kingdom to the Vespers of 1282, then the island kingdom (Trinacria, House of Barcelona). The Peace of Caltabellotta (1302) is the treaty that divided the old kingdom, but the land is split in 1282 because the revolt was the break de facto. The mainland that stayed with Charles of Anjou is kingdom-of-naples (Cliopatria row from 1285).
- Cliopatria gaps: no Kingdom of Sicily shape for 1285-1304 and only a 548 km2 scrap in Greece for 1279-1284, so the Angevin mainland 1282-1284 and the island 1285-1304 are blank, apart from the Crown of Aragon row's island cells in 1290-1304. The kingdom-of-naples dossier should start in 1282, or the colour agent should carry the mainland colour over. The dossier's new keyframes are _1130, _1137, _1144, _1146, _1155, _1282 and _1385; Africa overlays run 1146-1160.
- Overlap: the Crown of Aragon row (eu-12) draws the island in 1385-1409, which this dossier keeps as the Kingdom of Sicily's; one of the two should give way, or Aragon should be drawn as a personal union.
- Shared Wikidata: House of Anjou-Sicily and Capetian House of Anjou (eu-10) share Q939200; House of Anjou-Sicily's 1279-1284 row is Angevin Albania, Corfu, Achaea and Malta, not the kingdom.
- Cliopatria's Duchy of Naples row runs to 1138, a year after the pages' absorption of 1137; the Principality of Capua row ends 1065 though the principality lasted to 1139.
- Page disagreements: the Kingdom page dates the Treaty of Mignano 25 March 1139 (Roger II's page implies about 25 July; the ledger carries both under one conflict); the Kingdom page ends the sovereignty in 1410, Cliopatria's row ends 1384, the Compromise of Caspe of 1412 made the union permanent.
- Not on the pages opened: the Hohenstaufen succession by Manfred (1258), the Angevin court at Naples, the Treaty of Villeneuve 1372 ('The division in the kingdom became permanent in 1372'), the Calabrian holdings after 1302 and Reggio's recapture, the extent of the African possessions. No ruler strip (no page lists the kings in order). Cliopatria records a personal union with the Holy Roman Empire for 1216-1259; no event is drawn.
- Counterparts without a dossier of this group: the Zirid dynasty and Almohad rows exist as dossiers; Tripoli's ruler and the African rebels are unnamed on the page.

## county-of-savoy (Cliopatria 1260..1539, 14 shapes)

- The pages make the County and the Duchy (1416 onward) one state, which the house-of-savoy dossier carries from 1559 and names county-of-savoy as its predecessor. This dossier runs 1003 to 1559: it adds the 257 years Cliopatria lacks (new keyframes _1003, _1046, _1218, _1272, _1355, _1388, _1401) and the French occupation after 1539 (_1536). The dissolve of 1559 is a hand-over; keep one colour.
- Method: the pages date what Savoy gained (Vaud 1218, Bresse 1272, Faucigny and Gex 1355, Nice 1388, Geneva 1401) but Cliopatria shows Vaud, Bresse, Faucigny, Gex and Geneva only in its 1402 shape. Each block is written as Cliopatria's own 1402 cells within a named department, canton or disc, so the blocks sum to the 1402 shape. Twelve cells of that shape (Isere 4, Vercelli 2, Savoie 2, Haute-Savoie 2, Turin 1, Biella 1) and the 25 Piedmont cells of 1395 are explained by no page opened (confidence-1 events).
- Page disagreements: the Canton of Vaud page puts the counts in Vaud from 1218 and Bern's annexation by 1536, while the Duchy page says Savoy lost its lands 'north and south-east of Lake Geneva' to the Swiss after the Burgundian Wars (undated) and Cliopatria keeps Vaud to 1539 (a 1475 cell loss only); the Italian War page says both that Piedmont went back to Savoy and that France acquired it; the County page dates Imperial immediacy 1313 in the text and 1331 in the infobox; the Amadeus VI page gives his death as 27 February and 1 March 1383.
- Not drawn (page silent): Peter II's and Philip I's gains of the 1260s-1280s, Bugey, the Principality of Achaea and Piedmont (1418), Vercelli (1427), the territorial effect of the Burgundian Wars, Geneva's and Chablais's loss in 1536, the Truce of Nice, the exiled duke's remnant, the Treaty of Lausanne (1564). Suzerainty: Charles IV moved Savoy from the Kingdom of Burgundy to the Kingdom of Germany in 1361 (the kingdom-of-arles dossier carries that event).
- No ruler strip (only fragments of the regnal list on the pages) and no capital named for 1003-1294 (Chambery from 1295).
- Counterparts without a dossier: county-of-geneva (suggested slug), March of Turin, the Bishops of Aosta.
- Gazetteer names missing (a disc or department stands in for each): Maurienne, Tarentaise, Chablais, Faucigny, Genevois, Bresse, Bugey, Gex, Viennois.
