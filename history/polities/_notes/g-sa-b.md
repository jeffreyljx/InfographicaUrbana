# Notes for gap batch g-sa-b (Sind, Rajputana, Gujarat, Himalaya, Kosala)

Written 2026-11-01. Six new dossiers, all validate (`validate_dossier.py --resolve`). Every page was read via WebFetch on 2026-11-01. No Cliopatria row exists for any of them.

## arghun-dynasty (1520-1554, 4 pages)
- Pages: Arghun dynasty, Shah Beg Arghun, Tarkhan dynasty, Langah Sultanate. The Arghun page's upper/lower Sindh wording contradicts itself (Thatta "upper", Bukkur "lower"); the whole of Sindh is drawn as Cliopatria's Samma outline of 1521.
- Multan 1528-1541 is the weak stretch: Arghun governor (Mirza Askari), "thrown out of the city", rebels under Sultan Mahmud II "independently", Langah page "merger with the Mughal Empire in 1530", Sher Shah 1541. The 1530 loss is a bridge (confidence 1) to counterpart "Langah rebels of Multan (Sultan Mahmud II)", which is not a slug. langah-sultanate ends 1528 while its own infobox says 1530.
- Needs a dossier from another group: tarkhan-dynasty (1554 to 1591 or 1593, the two pages disagree; Mughal dossier cites "Jani Beg (Sindh)" for 1591). The Arghun family's earlier Kandahar/Zamindawar principality (Dhu'l-Nun Beg killed 1507; Babur took Kandahar 1522) has no dossier.
- Capital Bukkur: gazetteer has no entry (Sukkur used). Wikidata id null.

## chahamanas-of-ranastambhapura (1192-1301, 3 pages)
- Pages: Chahamanas of Ranastambhapura, Siege of Ranthambore (1301), Hammiradeva. Drawn as a 60 km disc round Ranthambore (the Chauhan dossier's handover), fort (50 km) lost 1226 and regained under Razia (1236-1240; first year used, confidence 1).
- Mamluk dossier has the 1226 gain but no loss of Ranthambore c. 1236-1240: a gap-filler should add it. Khalji dossier's 1301 gain is a coordinate disc (80 km); the 1301 loss here matches it.
- Chauhan dossier calls Govindaraja's Ranthambore a Ghurid "fief"; the page says he "agreed to rule as a vassal of the Ghurids in 1192" at Ajmer and then "established a new kingdom" at Ranastambhapura.
- Hammira's raids (Bhimarasa, Mandalgarh, Dhar, Abu, Pushkar) are undated and not drawn; the page lists him as "(1283-1311)" but ends the dynasty in 1301.

## chalukyas-of-lata (970-1070, 3 pages)
- Pages: Chalukyas of Lata, Chaulukya dynasty, Bhoja. The dynasty's page (c. 970-c. 1070, five rulers) and the other ledgers (Chaulukya to 1007, Kalyani Chalukyas 1007-1008, Chaulukya 1008-1009, Lata Chalukyas 1009-1018, Paramaras 1018-1056, Kalachuris to 1074) disagree on who held Lata. The dossier gives the map only 1009-1017 as the dynasty's own (Chaulukya loss 1009, Paramara gain 1018) and keeps the line to the infobox's c. 1070 as feudatories. The 970 found event will conflict with the Chaulukya cells; the Chaulukyas list the Lata Chalukyas as predecessor, so rule R2 should go to the Chaulukyas.
- No capital on the page (Bhrigukachchha appears only as a place Vigraharaja marched to): identity.capitals is empty. The 1009 gain is a bridge (confidence 1) copied from the Chaulukya ledger. Kirtiraja's reign is c. 1010-1030 on the dynasty page but he is defeated c. 1008 on the Chaulukya page.

## hunza-and-nagar (1200-1947, 4 pages)
- Pages: Hunza (princely state), Nagar (princely state), Hunza-Nagar Campaign, Gilgit-Baltistan. Two states (Hunza c. 1200, Nagar c. 1300) kept as one dossier because four dossiers cite one slug and one region (74.7 E, 36.35 N, 70 km). Hunza is the 56 km disc (infobox 10,000 km2), the pair the 70 km disc (13,400 km2 against 14,100).
- Ends at accession to Pakistan (Hunza 3 Nov, Nagar 18 Nov 1947, Gilgit-Baltistan page); the states survived inside Pakistan to 1972 (Nagar) and Sept 1974 (Hunza). Their tributary relations are not in the ledger types for a vassal: China from c. 1760 (Hunza), the Dogras 1860 (Hunza), the Kashmir Durbar to 1947 (Nagar), British suzerainty 1891; Qing and Kashmir dossiers should carry them.
- Gazetteer lacks Hunza, Nagar, Baltit/Karimabad.

## kangra-state (c. 1050-1809, 3 pages + 6 in the 2026-11-01 repair)
- Pages: Kangra State, Sansar Chand, Amar Singh Thapa; repair: Kangra Fort, Katoch, Rait (Himachal Pradesh), Guler State, Chamba State, Nurpur State. Founding year is a bridge for "11th century" [citation needed]; the Katoch page gives no date either (Mahabharata tradition; originator Rajanaka Bhumi Chand). No area or boundary on any page.
- Repair (reviewer verdict fail, all three fixes applied): (1) Shapes are now the 60 km disc round the fort cut to Himachal Pradesh and minus 30 km round Chamba town (32.56 N 76.13 E) and 20 km round Nurpur town (32.30 N 75.88 E): 27 cells, checked with gazetteer.py (the plain disc has 39). Chamba State page: capitals Bharmour (580-920) and Chamba (920-1948), so Chamba town is that state's seat. The Nurpur coordinates are the reviewer's; the Nurpur page names no seat. Guler (Guler State page: "founded in 1405 by Raja Hari Chand, a scion of the ancient royal family of Kangra", annexed by the Sikhs 1813) is not separated; no page gives a boundary. I also applied the cut to /identity/successors/0/land and the 1809 loss and dissolve regions (intersected with Sikh Empire 1820: 25 cells instead of 37), which the reviewer did not list, so the end events agree with the shape; sikh-empire/ledger/5 still uses the plain 60 km disc, so its owner should revisit it. (2) Keyframes: 1050-1619, 1620-1782 (minus the 25 km fort disc), new 1783-1788 (minus a 5 km disc, one cell, round the fort), 1789-1809. Ledger: new 1783 gain from mughal-empire (confidence 2; Kangra State page: Sansar Chand "ousted the then ruler of Kangra, Saif Ali Khan ... This happened in 1783, and Sansar Chand was aided by the Kanhaiya misl"), 1789 fort recovery now counterpart sikh-confederacy at confidence 2. (3) Capital: Kangra fort 1050-1620 and 1789-1809, a reading (no page names a capital).
- Disagreement with the reviewer's premise: the reviewer wrote that the Kangra State page "does not name the 1783-1789 holder". True of that page, but the Kangra Fort page does: the fort "was surrendered in 1783 by his son to the Sikh leader, Jai Singh Kanhaiya" and "In 1786, Raja Sansar Chand gained Kangra fort by peaceful treaty with Jai Singh Kanhaiya" (for territorial concessions in the Punjab). That page also has the Mughal governor Saif Ali Khan dead before the surrender and never mentions 1789. I kept 1789 (Kangra State page, which the reviewer confirmed) and put 1786 in the note; a gap-filler should open the Kanhaiya misl page and settle 1786 against 1789. The 1783 gain draws only the land round the fort (25 km minus 5 km), because the fort went to the Kanhaiya misl; the extent is my reading. No Kanhaiya misl dossier exists; sikh-confederacy (ends 1799) has no Kangra event.
- Not fixed, by design: Mughal vassalage of the Katoch rajas 1620-1783 (Kangra State: "reducing the Katoch rajas to the status of vassals"; Katoch page: "brought under the Mughal suzerainty by the emperor Jehangir"; Kangra Fort page: Dharam Chand submitted to Akbar in 1556 and agreed to pay tribute). The schema keeps vassal_start in the suzerain's ledger, so it is a gap in mughal-empire (which has no Kangra event at all: no 1620 gain, no vassal_start, no 1783 loss). Sansar Chand's expansion over neighbouring hill states stays undated and unnamed (the Kangra Fort page adds only "In 1805 he turned his attention to Bilaspur"); Kangra Fort has the fort besieged in 1807 and "most of the land of Kangra jagir" in Nepal by early 1809, which I put in the 1806 contest note.
- Rehlu 1794: the Sansar Chand page says "near present-day Rait village"; the Rait page gives no coordinates (17.5 km from Dharamshala, 7.5 km from Gaggal airport; Raj Singh "died on the battlefield at Nerti"), and the gazetteer lacks Rait and Dharamshala, so the region stays the state's own core (confidence 1). Add Rait to the gazetteer and the gain can be a radius there.
- Capital 1621-1788 is empty: Sujanpur_Tira and Sujanpur,_Himachal_Pradesh did not load (url_not_found). The Sansar Chand page has Nadaun only as one of his forts and where he "spent his last days".
- The Sansar Chand page says the Gorkhas "could not capture Kangra Fort"; the Amar Singh Thapa page says they "ultimately captured the fort". The Sansar Chand infobox (1758-1824) and text (c. 1765-1824) disagree; the Katoch page says r. 1775-1823; no ruler strip. Infobox gives the Sikh annexation as 1810, body text the treaty as 1809.
- Needs dossiers from another group: Chamba (late 6th century [citation needed] to 1948; Rehlu counterpart), Nurpur (1064 to 1815/1849), Guler (1405 to 1813). Gazetteer lacks Kangra/Nagarkot, Rait, Dharamshala, Sujanpur Tira.

## panduvamshi-dynasty (620-790, 2 pages)
- Pages: Panduvamshis of Dakshina Kosala (the article title; "Panduvamshi dynasty" does not load), Somavamshi dynasty. Reign years are Shastri's c. chronology (Tivaradeva disputed). Founding 620 and end 790 are bridges (confidence 1) for "c. 7th century" and "no record after Balarjuna".
- Nala dossier lists this dynasty as Nala successor (c. 580); the Dakshina Kosala page has the territory later "ruled by the Nalas, the Banas and the Kalachuris". Somavamshi dossier says the Kalachuris drove the Panduvamshis out; the Somavamshi page calls that "one theory", undated, and has a later Panduvamshi capital at Vinitapura (Binka). Land 790-882 is nobody's. An earlier Panduvamshi line of Mekala has no dossier; sharabhapuriya-dynasty (ended c. 590) has none.
- janapada-of-kosala is the ancient Kosala janapada, not this state. Gazetteer lacks Sirpur, Mahasamund, Dakshina Kosala.
