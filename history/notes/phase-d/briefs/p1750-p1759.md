## 2. What to look at

Your window is moderately busy (10 years with changes, 244 captions, 43 keyframe snaps). Spend your pages on the biggest problems the images and the packet show.

1. **The picture.** Render the first and last year of your window, and the busiest years from the packet, up to 6 images in all. Use `--compare-cliopatria` on at least two of them, and a bbox wherever the action is regional. Look for:
   - a state far outside its region, or a speckle of cells in the wrong place;
   - a state missing that the period's maps show, or one drawn after it ended;
   - a sudden jump that no event explains;
   - holes inside a state;
   - land drawn as one state's when it was its neighbour's;
   - a vassal or member drawn as independent when it was not, or the reverse;
   - a contested or occupied zone that is missing, or one that never ends.

   Cliopatria is not always right; the compiled map often corrects it on purpose. The question is whether the compiled map matches what the pages say.
2. **The packet's flags,** biggest first: conflicts of 300 cell-years or more, snaps that move 200 cells or more, skipped events and unresolved regions. A conflict where the smaller state rightly won needs nothing.
3. **Colours:** note the clashing neighbour pairs the packet lists, and any you see in the images. Report them; the colour agent fixes them.

## 3. How to correct (the patch file)

For each problem worth fixing, find out what happened from pages you open with WebFetch in this run: the state's article, the war, treaty or campaign page. Your page budget for the whole window is up to 8 pages. Never date or place anything from memory. If the pages are silent, leave it, and say so in your report.

Then write the smallest correction into `history/data/patches/p1750-p1759.json`, in the format of `data/patches/README.md`:

- **`events`:** a missing gain, loss, contest, occupation or vassal relation, written exactly as a dossier ledger event plus `slug`. Use it for a snap the ledger does not explain, and for land that changes hands with no event.
- **`keyframe_overrides`:** a keyframe drawn too wide or too narrow. Give it a new verdict and a `correction` region spec, using `minus` to cut land the state did not hold.
- **`rulings`:** a conflict that the size rule settled wrongly. Name the years, both states and the winner, with an optional region and status (vassal, constituent, occupied or contested).

Every entry carries a `source` URL you opened and a note or `why` that quotes or closely paraphrases the dated sentence. Keep the file to your own window's years. An entry that reaches across the boundary into the next window is applied, but tell the orchestrator under `problems`.

After writing the file, check it in two steps (`data/patches/README.md`, "Checking your patch"):

```
python3 -I -B /mnt/project-files/history/tools/compile_dossiers.py --check-patch p1750-p1759
python3 -I -B /mnt/project-files/history/tools/compile_dossiers.py --trial p1750-p1759 --stage /tmp/claude-0/work/window-p1750-p1759/stage
python3 -I -B /mnt/project-files/history/tools/render_year.py <year> --from /tmp/claude-0/work/window-p1750-p1759/stage [--bbox ...] --out /tmp/claude-0/work/window-p1750-p1759/<name>-after.png
```

- **`--check-patch`** takes a few seconds and validates every entry. Fix every `REJECTED` line.
- **`--trial`** takes about a minute and about 1 GB. It compiles up to your window with your patch into your private stage folder and publishes nothing. It prints:
  - your patch rows;
  - the conflicts and snaps that changed, before and after.

  Run it once per round of corrections, not after every entry.
- **`render_year.py --from`** draws the corrected year from your stage, so you can look at it again.

Every entry must end up applied.

Some things a patch cannot fix:
- a state's founding or end date is wrong;
- a whole state is missing;
- two dossiers are one state.

List these under `dossier_requests` in your report, with the page.
