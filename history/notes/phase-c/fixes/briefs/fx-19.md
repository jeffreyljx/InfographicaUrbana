## 1. What the findings mean

- **conflict** (from `notes/phase-c/conflicts.csv`): two dossiers claim the same cells in the same years, and no event settles it, so the compiler gave the cells to the smaller state ("size rule"). That is often right: a big empire's outline drawn over a neighbour it never ruled. It is wrong when the bigger state really held the land, or when the smaller one's shape is too wide. `notes/phase-c/conflict_places.csv` says, per conflict year, where the disputed cells are (admin-1 regions, centroid, bbox), who won, and which keyframe or ledger event each side claimed them by.
- **snap** (from `notes/phase-c/keyframe_snaps.csv`): in a keyframe's start year the dossier's ledger had drawn the state more than 20 cells or 5 percent away from that keyframe, so the compiler set the state to the keyframe in one jump. The ledger is missing the gains or losses between the previous keyframe and this one, or the keyframe itself is wrong. `notes/phase-c/snap_places.csv` gives the places added and removed, the previous keyframe and how many ledger events lie between.
- **hierarchy_fix** (from `notes/phase-c/hierarchy-fixes.csv`): a classifier that read both dossiers (without opening pages) proposed a correction while deciding which state sat inside which. Verify it before applying it; it may be wrong.

`history/data/hierarchy.csv` already makes the compiler draw a vassal or member state inside its overlord (rule R3: the child owns its cells, tinted as vassal or constituent of the parent). So **do not add mirror `vassal_start`/`vassal_end` events only to get the tint**; skip a finding that asks for nothing else, with the reason "hierarchy.csv supplies it".
