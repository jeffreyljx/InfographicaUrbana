### 2026-10-12 02:32 UTC · Suburban and commuter rail are excluded
- Answers: 02:31
- Decision: Jeffrey's ruling becomes a hard rule in the definition. Lines of suburban, commuter, regional or mainline railway networks (S-Bahn, RER, JR, Korail commuter services, Cercanías, CPTM, Metrorail in South Africa and the like) are not metro, even when they run frequent, fully segregated service.

A system's name alone never decides it, so "Metrorail" and "Metro Trains" commuter networks stay out.

### 2026-10-12 02:40 UTC · Definition approach (draft; finalised in the 03:10 entry below)
- Answers: 02:27
- Base: UITP's 2025 definition of a metro ("high capacity urban guided transport systems, mostly on rails, powered with electricity, running on an exclusive right-of-way, with trains composed of a minimum of two cars and with a total capacity of at least 100 passengers per train"), together with Vuchic's right-of-way category A (no level road or pedestrian crossings).
- Unit: each line is tested on its own. A system qualifies when at least one of its lines does, and only qualifying lines go in the database.
- Technology: neutral. Steel wheel, rubber tyre, monorail and maglev all count if the line passes every test. Each line is tagged with its class (metro, light metro, monorail and so on), so the map can filter.
- History: the test applies through time, so steam-hauled and cable-hauled urban railways from before electrification (London 1863, Glasgow 1896) count from their opening. Closed lines are kept, with their closing dates.
- Where: "Defining a metro system"; edge cases are being checked before the rules are fixed.

### 2026-10-12 03:10 UTC · Definition of a metro system, v1.0
- Answers: 02:27, 02:31
- Decision: a line is a metro line only if it passes seven tests, applied in this order. The first failure decides.
  - T1: a guided public passenger line.
  - T7: part of a metro network, not suburban, commuter, regional or mainline rail (Jeffrey's rule).
  - T6: urban service, not a single-site people mover. It needs at least 3 stations and 2 km. Airport links count only if they are part of a metro system or have two or more intermediate city stations. Circulators whose stations all lie within 1.5 km of the centroid are out.
  - T3: a segregated right-of-way, with at most 10 level crossings where trains have absolute priority.
  - T4: track reserved for metro trains (90 % or more of trains) between the line's first and last interchanges. Shared outer sections stay part of the line, tagged.
  - T2: trains of 2 or more cars and 100 or more passengers.
  - T5: every 15 minutes or better off-peak on the busiest section, all day, every day.
- A system qualifies when one of its lines does, and only qualifying lines go in the database.
- The database records systems, lines (with permanent ids), segments (the route between adjacent stations, drawn once), stations (interchanges counted once), opening dates (first day of public service) and closing dates. Each is matched to a ScraperMap city or given a new city record. "Major" is a filter for the map, not a test.
- History: steam- and cable-hauled lines count from opening (London 10 Jan 1863, Chicago 6 Jun 1892, Glasgow 14 Dec 1896), and so do predecessors that passed every test (Vienna's electric Stadtbahn from 4 Jun 1925). Closed lines keep their closing dates, and closures of a year or more are recorded as gaps. A line enters at the earliest date the evidence supports.
- Technology-neutral: monorails, AGT and maglev lines that pass are in, with `class` and `technology` tags so the map can filter them. **Superseded at 05:30 UTC: monorails are out (see "Monorails and skyrail excluded").**
- Section 9 of the definition rules on about 60 hard cases. Where the result differs from Wikipedia's List of metro systems, section 10 says so: London from 1863, New York's Els, Frankfurt U4, the Tokyo, Osaka, Las Vegas and Wuppertal monorails in; the Rinkai Line, Minatomirai Line and Disneyland Resort line out.
- Why: built on UITP's 2025 definition, Vuchic's right-of-way categories, the US NTD and Wikipedia's list, with numeric thresholds so each case can be decided. Identity decides the commuter-rail boundary because no physical test separates the Berlin S-Bahn or Yamanote line from Seoul Line 1.
- Where: "Defining a metro system"; [metro-definition.md](metro-definition.md).

### 2026-10-12 19:45 UTC · Master table format
- Answers: 19:18 ("Build a master csv or table of format of your liking")
- Decision: a small set of linked CSV files in `metro/db/`, keyed by permanent ids, plus GeoJSON for geometry:

- Why: one flat sheet can't hold lines, their stages and their stations without repeating rows; linked files keep each fact once and load straight into the map.
