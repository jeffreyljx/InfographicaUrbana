# World metro database (ScraperMap metro history mode)

_Built by the thread "Metro systems database", 12 Oct 2026 (all eleven regional passes in, 22:25 UTC); rebuilt 13 Oct 2026 with the fixes the Metro history map thread asked for (wrong-city matches, interchanges, doubled track, station dates); 19 Oct 2026, Ningbo Line 12 added (opened 22 Sep 2026; in as an exception to T5 on Jeffrey's ruling, definition 9.5), from the regional row in metro/tools/workers/w12-ningbo-12.*. What counts as a metro is decided by [metro-definition.md](../metro-definition.md); every instruction and design choice is logged in [metro.md](../metro.md)._

## Files

| File | One row per | What it holds |
|---|---|---|
| `metro_lines.csv` | line | **The master table.** System, city, country, ref, names, colour, termini, ruling (included or excluded, and the failing test), status, first opening, latest extension, closing, length (as published, as measured along one direction of OpenStreetMap's longest route, and as the total of one track of every branch), stations (published and mapped), annual ridership (with year and basis), rolling stock, car type, cars per train, power supply, gauge, automation, technology, express tag, national mode, operator, tunnel and bridge shares, the OpenStreetMap relations and Wikidata ids it was matched to, geometry status, sources and notes. |
| `metro_systems.csv` | system | Ruling, Wikipedia's totals (opening year, stations, length, annual ridership), and counts of included, excluded and mapped lines. |
| `metro_stages.csv` | dated event of a line | Opening of the first section, each extension, closure, reopening, infill station, transfer between lines, renaming: date, from and to stations, length, stations added, source. This is what the animation steps through. |
| `metro_stations.csv` | station on a line | Station id, line id, order along the line, name (local and English), latitude and longitude, opening date and how it was found (`opened_basis`), the ScraperMap city it stands in, OpenStreetMap ids, Wikidata id and start date where OpenStreetMap has them. An interchange appears once per line, with the same station id on every line. |
| `metro_segments.csv` | stretch between two adjacent stations of a line | From and to station, their coordinates, the opening date, and how it was found (`opened_basis`). This is the join of stages and stations that the animation draws from. |
| `metro_lines.geojson` | line | Path coordinates: the track of each line as a MultiLineString: one track per corridor (the opposite direction and other routes over the same track removed), with branches, with the line's id, name, colour and dates. |
| `metro_stations.geojson` | station | Station points (an interchange is one point, at the middle of its stop areas) with the lines that serve them. |
| `_report.json`, `_osm_unmatched.csv` | — | Coverage figures, included lines still without a path, and OpenStreetMap metro lines not yet matched to a line row. |

## Ids
- System: `<country code>-<system>`, e.g. `cn-beijing-subway`, `gb-london-underground`.
- Line: `<system_id>:<ref>`, e.g. `cn-beijing-subway:1`, `us-new-york-city-subway:a`, `us-chicago-l:humboldt-park-branch`. Ids are permanent and never reused.
- Station: `<system_id>:<OpenStreetMap stop area or node>`, e.g. `cn-beijing-subway:sa1234567`. Where OpenStreetMap maps an interchange as several stop areas, they share one id: stop areas of the same system with the same Wikidata id within 600 m, or the same name within 200 m, are merged.

## Sources and method
- **Systems**: Wikipedia's *List of metro systems* (read 12 Oct 2026), plus the systems the definition adds (Liverpool Overhead Railway, Frankfurt U4, Seville, and the Spanish and Tyne and Wear lines it marks for testing). Rulings follow metro-definition.md, including the cases this pass settled in its section 9.5.
- **Line facts and opening stages**: eleven regional passes over each system's and each line's Wikipedia article (English, and the local-language Wikipedia where it is better), copying figures as published, with the URL on every row. Unknown values are left blank, never estimated.
- **Path coordinates and stations**: OpenStreetMap, planet file of 8 Oct 2026, read from its public copy on Amazon S3. Each line's route relations (route=subway, light_rail, and metro-run train routes) give its track ways and its stops in order; stop areas group platforms and stop positions into one station. Lines were matched to OpenStreetMap by the Wikipedia article both cite, then by the route's network, operator and name tags, then by station names shared with the line's stages, then by line number, name and colour within the system. Every route must lie within 80 km of its system's city, so a route tagged with a generic name ("Metro", "Metrorail") can't land on a line in another city. Train routes need a match by Wikipedia, line number or name (shared stations alone are not enough, since through-running commuter trains share them), and light rail routes that share only stations with a subway line are dropped.
- **Opening dates of stations and segments**: each stage's from and to stations were found on the line's mapped station sequence (matching names in local script, English and common abbreviations), and every station and segment between them takes that stage's date (the earliest stage wins); `opened_basis` is `stage`. Where no stage covers a station: a line with a single stage dates all its stations (`line_single_stage`); a station between two stations of the same date takes that date (`between_stations_of_same_date`); otherwise OpenStreetMap's own start date is used if it isn't before the line opened (`osm_start_date`). A segment without a stage takes the later opening date of its two end stations (`end_stations`).
- **Length**: `length_km` is the published route length; `length_km_osm` is measured along the longest mapped route of the line; `track_km_osm` is one track of every mapped branch added up, so it is closest to the published figure for branched lines.

## Known gaps (this version)
- **Thin regions.** The shared Wikipedia fetch limit (400 pages an hour) cut several regional passes short, so smaller systems, mostly in China, India and Iran, rest on one or two pages and have empty fields.
- **Ridership per line** is published for about a third of the lines. Where only a system total exists, it is in `metro_systems.csv`.
- **Lengths.** `length_km_osm` is the longest mapped route of the line, so it runs long where OpenStreetMap's route includes express or through services (Chicago Purple Line Express, Beijing Line 4 with the Daxing line) and short where branches are mapped as separate routes (Stockholm, the DLR); `track_km_osm` adds those branches.
- **Closed lines** (New York's and Chicago's demolished elevated lines, the Liverpool Overhead Railway and others) have dates and stages but no path, because OpenStreetMap doesn't map demolished track. Their paths need historical sources.
- **Opening dates of stations and segments** come from the stages each line's Wikipedia article lists, with the fallbacks above. 2,656 of the 19,323 station rows of included lines are still undated (blank `opened`), mostly on lines whose stages name stations differently from OpenStreetMap.
- **Lines without a path.** 12 open included lines have no OpenStreetMap match yet: Los Teques Metro lines 1 and 2, the Hangzhou–Haining intercity line, Caracas line 5, Tehran line 5, Tashkent's O'zbekiston line, Brasília's orange line, Rome B1, Amsterdam 51, and the through-running Kobe Hokushin and Kita-Osaka Kyuko lines (mapped under neighbouring lines).
- **Merged lines.** A line whose identity ended when it joined another (Beijing's Batong line into Line 1, Hong Kong's Ma On Shan line into the Tuen Ma line, Ankara's M2 and M3 into M1) is `closed` on the merger date, with its track still in service under the line it joined. Ankara M1's path is still only its original Kızılay–Batıkent section.
- Rulings marked **check** in metro-definition.md section 9.5 are defaults until the fact is confirmed.
