## 2. What the sources say (quotes)

### 2a. Wikipedia, "List of cities with the most skyscrapers"
URL: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_with_the_most_skyscrapers

Lead: "For this article's purposes, a skyscraper is defined as 'a continuously habitable high-rise building that is taller than 150 meters (492 feet)'." ... "This list encompasses every city with more than 30 skyscrapers exceeding 150 meters as of February 2026. Unless otherwise noted, data derives from the Skyscraper Center database, managed by the Council on Vertical Urbanism (CVU). The CVU's figures may undercount actual skyscraper numbers and are unreliable for many non-Western cities due to incomplete high-rise building data."

- [b] "This figure is from the corresponding linked article."
- [c] "The CVU counts Sydney and Parramatta as separate cities. This figure is obtained from combining its figure for Sydney and Parramatta."
- [e] "This figure is from the linked page of Taipei's tallest buildings, which includes skyscrapers in both the municipalities of Taipei City and New Taipei City."
- [f] "The CVU's 'Explore Data' search function limits outputs to 25 buildings for free users."
- [g] "CVU figures without a 'greater than' sign (>=) should still be taken as minimum values."

### 2c. Wikipedia, "List of tallest buildings in Metro Manila"
URL: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_tallest_buildings_in_Metro_Manila
Lead: "As of November 2025, Metro Manila has 215 buildings that are confirmed to be 150 meters (492 feet) or higher." ... "This list ranks the highest completed skyscrapers and buildings in Metro Manila ... based on standard height measurement according to Emporis and CTBUH (unless otherwise stated, the two sources agree on the height of a building)." The height column is labelled "Height (Emporis)" - i.e. the list was built on the now-defunct Emporis database (shut down September 2022), which had far broader coverage of Philippine residential condominiums than CTBUH.
Also: "Not included in the list are buildings which have heights that are only estimated based on floor counts", with the page citing 70+ completed buildings with unverified heights - which is how the lead's 215 can exceed the table's 162 rows (162 ranked + ~53 height-unverified/other = 215; the 215 headline is therefore not a count of verified, completed >= 150 m buildings).
Sections: Tallest buildings (162 rows), Twin towers (16), Under construction or preparation (40+), Planned 200 m+ (6), Historical.

## 3. Why the Wikipedia figures exceed CTBUH's

1. Different database, not different buildings. Since 2025 the Wikipedia "most skyscrapers" table mixes two sources: the CVU cities page (Seoul 86, Busan 77, Hong Kong 569 ...) and, for cities flagged [a]/[b], the total stated in the corresponding "List of tallest buildings in X" article. Those city articles are crowd-compiled from Emporis (defunct 2022; the Manila list's height column is literally headed "Height (Emporis)"), SkyscraperPage/developer data and local press, and the Bangkok and Seoul leads say explicitly that only 129 of 204 and 82 of 126 respectively are in the CTBUH database.
2. Topped-out and unverified buildings. Note [a] states that the [a]-flagged figures "include topped-out buildings that are not fully completed"; Manila's 215 headline additionally counts ~53 buildings that do not appear in the article's own ranked table (rows are only added when a verified height exists; the lead counts height-estimated buildings too). CVU counts Completed only.
3. Residential-complex towers. The overwhelming majority of the "extra" buildings in Seoul (Tower Palace, Lotte Castle SKY-L65, Trimage, Acro Seoul Forest, Raemian, Prugio, Hyperion, Trapalace ...) and Bangkok (Park Origin, Ashton, Noble, Ideo, Rhythm, The Line, Skyrise Avenue, Belle Grand ...) are individual apartment/condominium towers in multi-tower complexes at 150-165 m. CTBUH relies on submissions "directly from companies involved in the projects" (FAQ); Korean apartment developers and Thai condo developers rarely submit, so CTBUH coverage of these is thin - the FAQ concedes "it is unlikely all tall buildings are represented on the Skyscraper Center", and the Wikipedia lead itself warns the CVU "may undercount ... and [is] unreliable for many non-Western cities".
4. Threshold sensitivity. Dozens of the extra towers are listed at exactly 150 m (Seoul has ~30 rows at 150 m). Height-to-architectural-top vs roof/estimate differences of 1-3 m decide inclusion, so any non-CTBUH list will be noisier around the threshold.
5. Geography. "Metro Manila" is not a CVU city; the Skyscraper Center splits it into Manila, Makati, Taguig, Pasig, Mandaluyong, Quezon City, San Juan, Pasay etc. Summing CVU rows gives 129-131, not 215. Likewise CVU splits Sydney/Parramatta and Seoul/Incheon.
6. The reverse case: Busan. Wikipedia's Busan list (~70) is below CVU's 77 because the article is under-maintained; CTBUH coverage of Busan's LCT/Marine City/Centum towers is comparatively good.

## 4. Recommendation: single consistent authority

Use the CTBUH/CVU Skyscraper Center cities page (Completed buildings, >= 150 m architectural height) as the spine for every city and district tally in this project, and record the fetch date with each number (the page is live: Shenzhen 468 -> 471, Dubai 269 -> 273 between Wikipedia's Feb-2026 snapshot and 2026-09-05).

Rationale: it is the only source with one definition (architectural height, Completed status), one geography convention, and global coverage; every other headline figure (Wikipedia, Guinness, press) ultimately cites either CTBUH or the defunct Emporis.

Caveats to state alongside every CVU-based figure:
- Under-coverage of residential complexes: Seoul (CVU 86 vs 126 documented completed towers, -32%), Bangkok (129 vs 204, -37%), Metro Manila (129-131 vs 162 verified-height rows, -20%). Treat CVU counts as minimum values (Wikipedia note [g] says the same). For Seoul, the 40 missing towers are almost all >= 150 m apartment towers in Dongdaemun, Yongsan, Seongdong, Yangcheon, Gangnam, Songpa, Jungnang; expect the CVU per-gu breakdown to be similarly skewed toward commercial districts (Yeongdeungpo/Yeouido, Gangnam, Songpa) and away from residential ones.
- Where a district-level (gu / khet / city) breakdown is needed, CVU's free tier cannot deliver it ("Explore Data" is capped at 25 rows / members-only). The Wikipedia city lists with a District column (Seoul, Busan, Bangkok, Metro Manila) are the only per-district sources, so use them for district *shares*, while anchoring city totals to CVU, and label the two series separately (never mix a Wikipedia district count with a CVU city total in one column).
- Metro Manila must be built up from CVU city rows (Makati, Taguig, Mandaluyong, Pasig, Quezon City, Manila, San Juan, Pasay, Paranaque, Muntinlupa); Pasay/Paranaque/Muntinlupa have <= 2 each and sit below the visible cut-off of the cities table - fetch their city pages individually or treat as 0-2.
- Do not use the Wikipedia "List of cities with the most skyscrapers" table as a source: since 2025 it is a mixed-provenance table (CVU for some cities, uncited city-article totals including topped-out buildings for others) and is internally inconsistent (Metro Manila 215 in the main table vs component-city CVU rows summing to 129 in its own metropolitan table).
- Hong Kong: CVU 569 (150 m+) is the consistent figure; Wikipedia's 554/567 are older snapshots of the same database. No live Guinness page could be retrieved to corroborate.
