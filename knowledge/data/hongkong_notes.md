## 3. The 569 vs 669 (and 567 / 552) reconciliation

### Where each number lives (quotes)
- **569** - en.wikipedia "List of cities with the most skyscrapers" (data "as of February 2026", source "Council on Vertical Urbanism (CVU) ... Skyscraper Center database" - CVU is CTBUH's 2025 rename): Hong Kong **569** >= 150 m, 102 >= 200 m, 6 >= 300 m, 2 >= 400 m. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_with_the_most_skyscrapers
- **569** - skyscraperatlas.com/city/hong-kong/: "as of 2025, Hong Kong has 569 buildings over 150 m". https://skyscraperatlas.com/city/hong-kong/ (no source cited; matches CTBUH exactly, so it is a CTBUH mirror).
- **567** - en.wikipedia "List of tallest buildings in Hong Kong" lead: "Hong Kong has over 9,000 high-rise buildings, of which over 1,000 are skyscrapers standing taller than 100 m (328 ft) with **567 buildings above 150 m** (492 ft) as of 2025", cited to CTBUH city listings (refs [2]-[4]; one ref is a legacy Emporis statistics page). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_tallest_buildings_in_Hong_Kong - simply an earlier 2025 snapshot of the same CTBUH count (567 -> 569 as Villa Garda etc. completed).
- **669** - Guinness World Records, "City with the most buildings 150 m or taller": Hong Kong, **669** buildings of 150 m or more, source "Council on Tall Buildings and Urban Habitat (CTBUH)", record dated **30 January 2025**; "Shenzhen ranked second with 626 buildings, followed by New York (434) and Dubai (424)". https://www.guinnessworldrecords.com/world-records/505189-city-with-the-most-buildings-150-m-or-taller
- (Separate Guinness record "Most skyscrapers in a city" = **2,580** buildings >= 100 m, CTBUH, March 2019 - a different threshold, not relevant to 150 m.) https://www.guinnessworldrecords.com/world-records/101147-city-with-most-skyscrapers
- **552** - appears in older Wikipedia/press snapshots ("more than 550 completed skyscrapers", Time Out HK, July 2025, citing CTBUH, "completed skyscrapers only"). https://www.timeout.com/hong-kong/news/hong-kong-has-the-most-skyscrapers-in-the-world-with-asian-cities-dominating-the-top-10-072925 - i.e. the same CTBUH completed count at an earlier date (2023-24).

### Why 669 differs from 569
Compare Guinness (Jan 2025) with the CTBUH/CVU *completed-only* counts on Wikipedia (Feb 2026):

| City | Guinness Jan 2025 | CTBUH completed Feb 2026 | Gap |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hong Kong | 669 | 569 | +100 |
| Shenzhen | 626 | 468 | +158 |
| New York | 434 | 324 | +110 |
| Dubai | 424 | 269 | +155 |

Every Guinness figure is *higher* than the later completed-only figure, by 100-160, in the cities with the most under-construction / proposed / on-hold 150 m+ projects. Since the completed count only grows over time, the Guinness numbers cannot be an older completed count; they must be a CTBUH query run with **all statuses** (completed + under construction + topped-out/architecturally topped out + on hold / proposed / never completed), which is what the Skyscraper Center's city "explore data" tool returns if you do not filter on status. For Hong Kong, ~15-20 towers under construction (LOHAS Ph12-13, Mong Kok East 312 m, Kai Tak, etc.) plus roughly 80 proposed / on-hold / never-completed / demolished entries (e.g. the Kowloon Station and Kai Tak visions, West Kowloon Cultural District towers, "Mega Tower" Wan Chai, Sun Hung Kai's cancelled schemes) account for the +100. It is *not* an Emporis figure (Emporis, which counted HK at ~ 300-400 "skyscrapers" under its own >= 100 m definition, shut down in 2022), and it is not a per-tower recount of podium-linked complexes (that would add far more than 100 - see below).

A secondary possibility is a plain transcription slip (569 -> 669) on Guinness's side, but the identical over-count pattern in the other three cities makes the all-statuses explanation far more likely.

### Per-tower vs per-row: the *other* gap you will see when you build district lists from CTBUH
CTBUH stores many podium-linked Hong Kong estates as one row per group: Le Prestige "Towers 2-5" (3 towers), Grand Marini/Ocean Marini (2), Ocean Shores "1-3", "9-10", "11-13", "16-17" (10 towers in 4 rows), The Arch (4 towers in 1 row), Harbourside (3 in 1), Hermitage "1-3"/"6-8", One SilverSea "1-8", Island Resort "1-2/3-5/6-7/8-9", Bellagio "1-5"/"6-9", Grand Promenade "2-5", Manhattan Hill "1-2", Royal Peninsula "2-5", Sun Tuen Mun Centre. In Sai Kung alone this is 110 towers vs 101 rows; in YTM 39 towers vs 23-30 rows. Citywide the per-tower count of completed 150 m+ towers is therefore well **above** 569 - likely 650-700 - so a district-by-district per-tower tally will land near the Guinness number by coincidence, not because it measures the same thing.

### Recommendation
1. **Use 569 (CTBUH/CVU, completed, Feb 2026) as the citywide spine** and state the status filter explicitly ("completed, architectural height >= 150 m, CTBUH row basis"). Cite Wikipedia's cities list and skyscraperatlas as mirrors; cite Guinness only as "669 incl. all statuses".
2. **Decide once whether the project counts rows or towers**, and apply it to every city. If the project counts towers (which the Guangzhou/Shanghai/Shenzhen CSVs in this Project appear to do), then the Hong Kong district sum will exceed 569 and should be reported as "N towers (CTBUH row-basis: 569)".
3. For district allocation of the 569, the district totals that dominate are Central & Western, Wan Chai, Eastern, Kwun Tong, Sham Shui Po, Kwai Tsing, Tsuen Wan, Sai Kung and YTM. On row basis, **Sai Kung = 101** (this audit) and **YTM = 23-30 verified, ~35-45 once the Olympic-station estates are checked**. The "missing 100" between a naive Wikipedia-table tally (166 rows >= 179 m) and 569 is almost entirely 150-179 m residential estates: Sai Kung (Ocean Shores, Park Central, TKO Plaza, Residence Oasis, Metro City I, East Point City = 40+ rows alone), Kwai Tsing/Tsuen Wan (Bellagio, Vision City, Indi Home, Ocean Pointe, Sham Wan), Sham Shui Po (Banyan Garden, Liberte, Manhattan Hill, Cullinan West), Kwun Tong (Laguna City-era 150 m+ towers are below, but Millennium City/Landmark East/One Kowloon are in), Eastern (Island Resort, Grand Promenade, Taikoo), Tuen Mun (Sun Tuen Mun Centre x10 at exactly 150 m), Kowloon City (Royal Peninsula). Any Hong Kong district tally must therefore pull CTBUH complex pages for the 150-179 m estates, not just the Wikipedia table.
