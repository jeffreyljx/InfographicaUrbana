# The World's Urban Districts With the Most Skyscrapers: A Building-by-Building Tally (≥150 m)

*Second edition — 05 September 2026. Every figure below is a count of individual buildings located one-by-one on a map; nothing in this edition is an estimate. Data: CTBUH / Council on Vertical Urbanism Skyscraper Center (live database, September 2026); locations: OpenStreetMap administrative boundaries (with geoBoundaries, Nominatim and targeted research as back-ups). Coverage: 5,950 completed buildings ≥150 m in 64 CTBUH cities (58 metropolitan units after grouping Metro Manila), of which 5,922 (99.5%) are assigned to a sub-city unit.*

## TL;DR
- **Manhattan is, by a wide margin, the administrative district with the most skyscrapers on Earth: 294 completed buildings ≥150 m (96 ≥200 m, 17 ≥300 m).** Its Midtown section alone (14th–59th St) holds 207 — more than any other district anywhere.
- **Shenzhen's Nanshan District (189) is the clear world #2, ahead of Shenzhen's Futian (127) and Hong Kong's Sai Kung District (122).** Nanshan also has the biggest pipeline on the list (25 topped-out + 18 under construction ≥150 m), so it is pulling further ahead; the earlier estimate of ~110 for Nanshan was far too low, and the estimated Futian/Nanshan order was reversed.
- **The rest of the top ten are Old Toronto (100), Shanghai's Pudong (92), Guangzhou's Tianhe (87), Hong Kong's Central & Western (79), Shenzhen's Luohu (76) and Singapore's Downtown Core (71), followed by Chicago's Loop (70).** Only five districts in the world have 100 or more buildings ≥150 m; 24 have 50 or more; 56 have 30 or more (out of 416 sub-city units with at least one).

## Key findings

**Manhattan's lead is real and not an artefact of counting method.** Manhattan has 294 completed buildings ≥150 m against 189 for Nanshan; even if Nanshan's entire pipeline (43 towers) completed tomorrow it would still trail. The 96 buildings ≥200 m in Manhattan are also more than any other district (Nanshan 79, Futian 59). Sub-dividing Manhattan by community-board lines, Midtown (14th–59th St) has 207 buildings ≥150 m, Lower Manhattan (south of 14th St) 65, the Upper East/West Side 21 and Upper Manhattan 1. Brooklyn (16) and Queens (14) complete New York City's CTBUH total of 324.

**Shenzhen, not Hong Kong, has the two strongest non-Manhattan districts.** Nanshan (189; 79 ≥200 m) and Futian (127; 59 ≥200 m) both outrank every Hong Kong district. Nanshan's count is driven by the Houhai/Shenzhen Bay and Qianhai office clusters plus large residential estates: 75 of its towers sit in Yuehai Sub-district alone (Houhai), 30 in Nanshan Sub-district (Qianhai side), 17 in Shekou. Luohu (76) is world #9. Shenzhen's outer districts are far smaller than previously guessed: Bao'an 30, Longhua 25, Longgang 11 (the earlier estimates of 60/40/50 were wrong).

**Hong Kong's towers are more evenly spread than its skyline suggests.** Sai Kung District (122 — almost entirely the Tseung Kwan O/LOHAS Park residential estates, 98% residential, median height 168 m) leads, then Central & Western (79), Yau Tsim Mong (55), Tsuen Wan (53), Wan Chai (42), Sham Shui Po (37), Sha Tin (31), Kwun Tong (28), Eastern (22), Islands (17). By region: New Territories 267, Hong Kong Island 154, Kowloon 145. Earlier estimates overstated Yau Tsim Mong and Eastern and understated Central & Western and Tsuen Wan.

**Several districts that were not on the earlier list rank in the global top 25:** Old Toronto (100, #5), Singapore's Downtown Core (71, #10), Kuala Lumpur's KLCC/Golden Triangle (64), South Jakarta (63), Makati (59), Zhuhai's Xiangzhou (56), Guangzhou's Haizhu (56), Beijing's Chaoyang (49), Chengdu's Wuhou (49), Nanning's Qingxiu (47) and Busan's Haeundae-gu (45).

**Dubai's clusters are smaller than assumed but taller:** Dubai Marina 64 (31 ≥200 m, 10 ≥300 m), Downtown Dubai 53, Jumeirah Lakes Towers 41, the Sheikh Zayed Road/Trade Centre strip 35, Business Bay 31, DIFC/Zabeel 17. The earlier estimate of 90–100 for the Marina was too high; the CTBUH-consistent figure is 64 (JBR towers are included where they fall inside the Marina community polygon).

**Counting method still matters for the residential-estate districts.** Sai Kung (98% residential), Dubai Marina (80%) and Tsuen Wan owe their ranks to repetitive estate towers; Manhattan (28% residential-only) and Nanshan (30%) are office-led. Both views are given in the tables (≥150 m and ≥200 m columns), and each building's function is in the spreadsheet.

**City totals reconcile to CTBUH.** The reconstructed completed counts equal CTBUH's published city figures exactly for 40 of 56 cities and are within 1–5 buildings for the rest (differences are buildings with no completion year or with functions CTBUH's own query tool cannot filter). The full reconciliation is in the spreadsheet.

## How the numbers were built (and what "district" means)

1. **Building list.** The live CTBUH/CVU Skyscraper Center database was queried through its public "Explore Data" tool for every in-scope city, slicing by completion year (and by function and material where a year had more than 25 buildings, the tool's display cap) until every completed building ≥150 m was listed with its exact height, floors, function and material. Buildings the tool cannot reach (no completion year, or a function outside its five filters) were recovered from the site's public search index and verified one by one on their building pages; 26 index records whose live height is 149.0–149.9 m and 23 with no confirmed height were excluded, matching CTBUH's own ≥150 m rule. Topped-out and under-construction buildings were collected the same way and reported separately.
2. **Coordinates.** CTBUH's own coordinates (from its search index) for 6,106 buildings; the remaining buildings were geocoded from their CTBUH street address (OpenStreetMap Nominatim) or located by targeted research.
3. **Map check.** Every coordinate was tested against OpenStreetMap administrative boundaries with an exact point-in-polygon query (Overpass `is_in`), which returns the full administrative hierarchy (district, sub-district, ward, community board…). geoBoundaries polygons were used as an independent second opinion and as the fallback where OSM boundaries are missing; Nominatim reverse geocoding provided a third check and the OSM building name (which matched the CTBUH name for a large share of buildings, confirming identity). Disagreements between sources were reviewed by hand; where they were real, OSM was preferred (its boundaries are current — e.g., Hangzhou's 2021 district reform, the 2023 Taguig/Makati ruling for BGC — whereas geoBoundaries lagged).
4. **Unit used per city.** Formal districts wherever they exist: Hong Kong's 18 districts; Chinese county-level districts (区); New York boroughs; Tokyo special wards and Osaka wards; Seoul/Busan/Incheon gu; Bangkok khet; Jakarta administrative cities; Mumbai BMC wards; Singapore URA planning areas; London boroughs; Moscow raions; Istanbul ilçe; Panama City corregimientos; Mexico City alcaldías; Chicago community areas; Toronto's pre-amalgamation municipalities (Old Toronto, Etobicoke, North York…); Melbourne and Sydney suburbs; Metro Manila's component cities (which CTBUH treats as separate cities). Where no formal sub-city unit exists (Dubai, Abu Dhabi, Doha, Kuala Lumpur, Miami, Houston, Los Angeles), buildings were grouped into the commonly used named areas/communities, and the grouping is stated in each table.
5. **Cross-checks.** District sums equal city totals by construction; city totals were reconciled to CTBUH's published city ranking; landmark buildings were spot-checked (ICC → Yau Tsim Mong, Ping An → Futian, Willis → Loop, Burj Khalifa → Downtown Dubai, Lotte World Tower → Songpa-gu, Shanghai Tower → Pudong/Lujiazui, One WTC → Manhattan CB1, Federation Tower → Presnensky, etc.); NYC borough counts were compared with Wikipedia's borough lists (Brooklyn 16 vs 15–17; Queens 14 vs 16 — the difference being 2025–26 completions CTBUH has not yet marked completed).

## What changed versus the first (estimated) edition

The city totals were already right; the district splits were not. The biggest corrections: Nanshan 189 (was ~110) and now #2 worldwide ahead of Futian 127 (was ~120); Bao'an 30 (was ~60) and Longgang 11 (was ~50); Central & Western 79 (was ~50) and Tsuen Wan 53 (was ~26) up, Yau Tsim Mong 55 (was ~80) and Eastern 22 (was ~50) down; Haizhu 56 (was ~25); Tokyo's Chiyoda 35 (was ~18); Bangkok's Watthana 12 (was ~35 — Pathum Wan 22 leads instead); Dubai Marina 64 (was 90–100) and Downtown Dubai 53 (was ~30); Chengdu's official districts are Wuhou 49 / Jinjiang 42 / Shuangliu 15 (the Hi-tech Zone and Tianfu New Area are functional zones overlapping them). Cities completed for the first time: Toronto, Moscow, Sydney, Singapore, Jakarta, Osaka, Istanbul, Busan, Incheon, Doha, Abu Dhabi, Panama City, Miami, Houston, Los Angeles, Mexico City, Macau, Balneário Camboriú and 25 further Chinese cities.

## Caveats

- **Names.** Buildings carry CTBUH's current official names (e.g., Merdeka 118 is listed as "Menara Merdeka Maybank"; Petronas Towers as "Petronas Twin Tower 1/2").

- **Authority.** All counts follow the CTBUH/CVU database and its rules (architectural height ≥150 m, status *Completed*; each tower counted separately, including near-identical estate towers). Wikipedia lists count more buildings in some cities (Hong Kong, Seoul, Bangkok, Mumbai, Metro Manila) because they include buildings CTBUH has not entered or verified; those are deliberately not mixed in.
- **Currency.** Snapshot of the live database on 05 September 2026. Buildings marked topped-out or under construction (listed separately) will move into the completed counts over the next 1–3 years; Nanshan (+43 in the pipeline), Futian (+16), Old Toronto (+16), Downtown Dubai (+9) and Business Bay (+10) will change most.
- **Reconciliation gaps.** Reconstructed city totals are 1–5 buildings below CTBUH's published figures for 16 cities (Hong Kong −3, Shanghai −3, Beijing −3, Zhuhai −5, Xi'an −3, Qingdao −3, others −1/−2), because a handful of buildings with no recorded completion year or an unusual function cannot be listed by CTBUH's own query tool; they do not affect any ranking position.
